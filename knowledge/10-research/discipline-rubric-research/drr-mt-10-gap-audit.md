**Final Handoff for MT Revision**

The Run 0B Musical Theatre slice is broadly sound as a **baseline-preserving** object, and the audit does not support structural upheaval. The current strengths that must not regress are the protected MT anchor itself: the existing six-field framework, visible acting and vocal categories, current MT weights, multi-component detection, component breakdown, and timestamp spread across acting and song where possible. Those are aligned with the strongest official and exam-board evidence and are also explicitly protected by the Run 0E guardrail pack. Core preserve items therefore sit around [MT-S004], [MT-S007], [MT-S045], [MT-S049], [MT-S013] and the 0E MT stabilisation rules.

The highest-priority improvements are about **specificity inside the existing structure**, not about new score fields or new weighting models. The most urgent audit focus is acting-through-song: MT feedback should not be allowed to praise or criticise song performance without citing lyric-, phrase-, or beat-level evidence. This is strongly supported by [MT-S004], [MT-S006], and [MT-S077]. Closely linked to this, the vocal area needs a clearer internal distinction between **technical accomplishment** and **communication/story/style**, using evidence from [MT-S007], [MT-S072], and [MT-S034]. Movement notes also need to become more defensible: if the tape does not show visible, assessable movement, the rubric should not invite generic dance praise. Strongest support there comes from [MT-S019], [MT-S070], and [MT-S051].

The second major audit priority is **integration**. Musical Theatre should not read like separate acting feedback and singing feedback placed side by side. The later MT revision should check whether the component breakdown, timestamps, and fix-first logic force at least one integration comment when acting and song coexist, grounded in scene-to-song continuity, acting continuity inside song, or movement in service of lyric/story. Key evidence sits in [MT-S004], [MT-S009], [MT-S045], and [MT-S049].

The third priority is **fairness, non-penalisation, and filtering**. Self-tape assessability must be separated from performance quality, and low-budget or access-adapted tapes must not be treated as weaker talent evidence unless assessability is materially blocked. That boundary is strongest in [MT-S055], [MT-S056], and [MT-S011]. Practitioner material should also be filtered: authenticity, individuality, preparation and direction-response are useful only where tied to observable evidence; body/type/appearance, passport/nationality, social media, charm, marketability, generic “presence,” and tape-only vocal-health diagnosis must stay out. This exclusion work is grounded in [MT-S055], [MT-S056], [MT-S064], [MT-S072], and the 0E anti-bias guardrails.

Subtype/style handling should be improved, but only conditionally. The evidence supports style-aware report language for Golden Age versus contemporary, legit/mix/belt, jukebox, and acting-led / voice-led / dance-led emphasis through [MT-S072], [MT-S078], [MT-S074], [MT-S070], and [MT-S077]. It does **not** support fixed subtype weightings, new subtype score fields, or a solved framework for through-composed MT. Through-composed remains an explicit evidence gap anchored by [MT-S066], [MT-S074], and [MT-S079], and the later revision must keep that limitation visible rather than filling it by inference.

In short, the later MT revision should: preserve the stabilised MT core, strengthen evidence requirements, make acting-through-song and integration structurally unavoidable in commentary, separate fairness from quality, capture subtype/style only where observable, suppress direction-response unless visible, exclude bias-sensitive material, and defer fixed weighting or deeper dance/voice design changes to later discipline-specific research.
