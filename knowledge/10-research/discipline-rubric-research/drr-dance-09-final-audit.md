**Final Audit Scope and Preservation Rules**

This final audit covers acceptance of the revised Dance wording, guardrails, addenda and traceability against the completed Dance synthesis and Dance audit. It tests whether the revision package materially answers the audited problems without destabilising the preserved shared system. It does not test live implementation behaviour, frontend label rendering, comparison-page category labelling, rendered/exported Dance reports, or real output execution, because those materials were not supplied in this run.

The following remain out of scope because of explicit architecture and implementation guardrails: backend logic, schema, score-field architecture, weights, caps, blockers, verdict thresholds, role-fit bounds, server-side recomputation, Mux or pipeline flow, Step 1 / Step 2 process shape, and the stabilised Musical Theatre acting-plus-song flow. Those constraints are explicitly preserved by the control materials and by the revision package itself. fileciteturn0file0 fileciteturn0file2 fileciteturn0file3

A return to DANCE-REV would be required only if this audit found that the revision package failed materially on one of the core high-priority audit targets: dance-only label containment, style-specific Dance language, tape assessability preconditions, live-room-only claim boundaries, accessibility-safe assessment, or protection of the shared-system / Musical Theatre non-regression envelope. On the supplied materials, that threshold is not met.

## Final Release Decision and Handoff

**Final Release Decision**

**DANCE-FINAL-D01 — Approved with explicit limitations**

The revised Dance baseline is materially compliant with the completed synthesis and materially responsive to the completed audit at wording-and-guardrail level. It now contains the dance-only vocal-proxy risk as far as the current architecture allows, introduces style-aware Dance handling, makes assessability a genuine precondition, blocks generic unsupported praise, ring-fences live-room-only capacities, strengthens accessibility-safe assessment, tightens professional-presentation boundaries, and adds a Dance-specific timestamp / reliability rule-set. None of the preserved shared-system constraints or Musical Theatre non-regression constraints are destabilised by the revision.

There are no critical blockers in the revised text that require a forced return to DANCE-REV before the next stage. The remaining issues are limitations, not release blockers at baseline-text level: partly structural vocal-proxy semantics, unverified frontend label-helper behaviour, unverified comparison-page label behaviour, unverified renderer/export note behaviour, limited commercial/street/hip-hop universality, thinner tap evidence outside formal frameworks, and the absence of live Dance report examples for spot-auditing.

**DANCE-FINAL-D02 — Approved for Output Specificity / Non-Regression Test Mapping with explicit limitation set**

The next stage can move forward on the basis of the accepted revision package. What must move forward explicitly are the test clusters around display-layer label containment, assessability preconditions, anti-polish boundaries, evidence-anchor enforcement, live-room-only overclaim suppression, accessibility-safe output language, commercial/street caution, tap audibility, and timestamp rendering / density. What must remain openly listed as limitations are DANCE-FINAL-I01 to DANCE-FINAL-I08.

**Reusable Handoff Pack for the Next Stage**

**DANCE-FINAL-AUDIT handoff summary**

The final audit confirms that the Dance revision package is materially fit for the next stage. The revised baseline now answers the highest-risk Dance problems at wording-and-guardrail level without disturbing the shared architecture that the control materials require TapeCoach to preserve. Most importantly, it contains the dance-only vocal-proxy problem in user-facing language, adds style-specific Dance handling inside the existing shared framework, and codifies assessability preconditions so that full-body or task-sufficient visibility, stable framing, readable space, audible music where relevant, continuity and simple capture are treated as fairness conditions, not aesthetic preferences.

The revised baseline also now makes clear that Dance reports must be evidence-led. Generic phrases such as good energy, strong technique, nice movement or strong performance are no longer acceptable unless they are anchored to visible movement, a task, a timestamp, a style-specific feature, or an assessability condition. Live-room-only capacities are also materially better protected: choreography pick-up, response to direction, adaptability, workshop behaviour and stamina are now marked as mainly live-room or callback evidence unless directly shown.

Accessibility-safe assessment is materially improved. The revised baseline now separates process adaptation from attainment and blocks deficit inference from mobility aids, seated adaptation, reduced range, convalescence, bespoke pathways or body difference. professional_presentation is also properly constrained: it now covers assessability, brief response and safe preparation, not glamour, polish, physique or expensive production.

The remaining risks are real but non-blocking. They are mostly the same as the ones already flagged in the audit and revision handoffs: the structural vocal-proxy issue remains partly architectural; commercial/street/hip-hop descriptors are less universal than ballet/contemporary/jazz/tap; tap remains thinner outside formal frameworks; and actual Dance report rendering, label-helper behaviour and output specificity were not directly auditable from the supplied materials. Those issues should now be handled through output-specificity and non-regression testing rather than another wording loop.
