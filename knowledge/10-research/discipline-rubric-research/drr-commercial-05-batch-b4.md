### 4. Source-by-Source Evidence Notes

#### COMMERCIAL-S025 — Building Confidence on Screen
- The page is a LAMDA short-course offer for professional or returning actors and frames itself around transferring stagecraft to screen, working on scripts, and developing a reliable self-taping process. citeturn2view0turn6view3
- It explicitly supports stage-to-screen adjustment, independent self-taping confidence, and general screen-craft language, but it does not mention Commercial-specific performance, product copy, brand handling, or Commercial subtype distinctions. citeturn2view0turn6view3
- It supports only very broad tape-observable language such as “effective self-tapes” and “successful screen performance”; those phrases are too generic to become Commercial criteria on their own. citeturn6view3
- Its strongest limitation is source shape: it is a dated course page with a 2023 deadline and an applications-closed notice, so it is best treated as low-confidence vocabulary support rather than current Commercial guidance. citeturn2view0

#### COMMERCIAL-S026 — Actor Refresh: Solo Preparation for the Screen Actor
- This LAMDA page is stronger than S025 on reusable solo preparation: it explicitly describes a reliable, reusable and independent solo preparation process for screen, fundamentals of self-taping, and one filmed self-tape-style scene as an example of camera acting. citeturn2view1turn6view0turn6view1
- It supports Commercial-adjacent language around solo-copy or independent preparation only in the generic sense of preparing for screen before arriving on set; it does not mention Commercial briefs, products, brands, point of view, or direct-to-camera Commercial handling. citeturn6view0turn6view2
- It supports tape-observable ideas only at a broad “camera acting / self-tape-style scene” level and is therefore not sufficient to define subtype-specific Commercial evidence. citeturn6view0turn6view2
- The source is clearly course-shaped and fee-based, so its process, method and “professional self-tape standard” language must not be imported as universal scoring logic. citeturn2view1turn6view0

#### COMMERCIAL-S027 — Working Actor Practice: intensive
- The RADA page is the thinnest B4 source for Commercial-specific use. It describes a professional workshop building agility with new material, strong creative choices, taking direction on and off camera, and techniques for auditions and self-tapes. citeturn2view2turn6view4
- That makes it relevant only as generic screen/audition vocabulary. It says nothing explicit about Commercial copy, product context, brand world, presenter-led work, UGC/social style, or voiceover-style reads. citeturn2view2turn6view4
- Nothing on the page safely supports tape-side scoring; almost all useful content is training/process language. citeturn2view2
- Its authority is institutional, but the page is promotional and course-shaped rather than evaluative, so it must remain low-confidence support only. citeturn2view2

#### COMMERCIAL-S028 — How to Analyze Voiceover Copy
- This is the strongest B4 source. Terry Berland explicitly frames standout reads as having a point of view and says the script itself contains the answers for performance choices. citeturn4view0turn9view1
- The article explicitly links performance tone to visual descriptors, written descriptions, product context, and cue words. It gives voiceover examples such as intimate reads for tight shots, upbeat reads for fast food, refined reads for luxury cars, and reassuring reads for financial services. citeturn4view0turn4view1turn9view0turn9view1
- It is most useful for voiceover-style Commercial or spoken-copy contexts where visuals or script descriptors are supplied. It does not support presenter-led, corporate/industrial, or UGC/social-style rules, and it does not give on-camera self-tape setup instructions. citeturn9view0turn9view1
- The limitation is major: it is one named casting professional’s voiceover process, so product-linked descriptors and point-of-view language must be treated as conditional, not universal. citeturn4view0turn9view3

#### COMMERCIAL-S029 — Commercial Voiceover: 10 Commercial Tones to Cultivate
- This Casting Networks article is overtly voiceover-specific. It says product type drives vocal personality and offers a list of product-linked tone families, such as calm/reassuring medical reads, dreamy sleep-aid reads, upbeat household reads, formal “professional” reads, and conversational younger-skewing reads. citeturn2view4turn5view0turn5view1turn5view2
- It therefore adds some pattern language for voiceover-style Commercials, especially product-linked tone and audience feel, but not for general on-camera Commercial acting. citeturn5view0turn5view4
- It also contains overt self-marketing language — describing your voice to align with opportunities — which is process/career advice rather than assessment evidence. citeturn5view1turn5view4
- The page itself says the list is not comprehensive, which is the clearest reason it must not become a fixed Commercial taxonomy. citeturn5view1

#### COMMERCIAL-S030 — Writing an Effective Commercial Voice Over Script
- This Voices page is a client-side chapter in an “Ultimate Guide To Hiring Voice Actors”, not an actor-scoring source. It focuses on how to write a commercial voiceover script that suits brand, message and audience. citeturn2view5turn7view1
- It does add low-confidence context for spoken-copy handling: write in a conversational voice, write for the spoken word, avoid jargon, keep paragraphs readable, match vocal direction to the script’s tone, write for a target audience, and communicate the brand/message concisely. citeturn7view0turn7view3turn7view4
- It also introduces hard-sell, medium-sell and soft-sell distinctions and a simple 30-second structure, but these are copywriting / campaign-shaping ideas, not performer-score descriptors. citeturn7view2turn7view3
- The key limitation is that the page is about helping clients and hirers shape scripts and briefs. It should only ever be used as low-confidence support for copy clarity or brief completeness, not for judging performers. citeturn2view5turn7view0
