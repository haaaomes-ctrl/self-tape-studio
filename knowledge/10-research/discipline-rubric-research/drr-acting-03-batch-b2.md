## Source-by-Source Evidence Notes

**ACTING-S029 — LAMDA Exams Acting**  
The public exam page and the 2024 syllabus explicitly frame acting as a performance qualification built around interpretive skills, technical skills and knowledge of process. The acting exam is available in solo, duologue and combined formats, with solo exams available online. The syllabus requires memorised scenes and, by level, moves from basic character/location questions to more advanced demands around text and subtext, character intentions and objectives, vocal control, diction, physical response, context and period/style understanding. It also permits some medium crossover: own-choice material can include published plays and feature films at several levels, while higher levels restrict own-choice scenes to post-1980 plays or feature films and explicitly exclude television serials and novel adaptations. The same syllabus also includes named-practitioner knowledge at higher levels, so it is useful as a descriptor source but must not be universalised into one acting-method standard. citeturn0view0turn11view0turn11view1turn11view2turn11view3turn11view4turn11view5turn13view0turn17view2

**ACTING-S030 — Trinity Acting**  
The public page and specification frame acting as a drama qualification centred on vocal, physical and characterisation skills, with solo, duo, group and digital options. Trinity’s acting specification distinguishes task types rather than treating acting as one undifferentiated performance event: monologues, duologues, original scenes and reflections are separate components. It also requires contrast by language style, genre and/or period at some grades, expects work from published sources unless stated otherwise, and at intermediate/advanced levels requires acting play extracts to come from theatre rather than film or television. The reflection component is important: candidates may be assessed on context, rehearsal process, voice/body choices, writing styles, contrast, pair collaboration and interpretation. That makes Trinity highly useful for separating tape-observable performance from non-performance knowledge, but it also means later Acting outputs should not imply that every contextual claim can be inferred from the finished tape alone. citeturn21view1turn9view0turn9view1turn9view2turn13view5turn13view6turn17view3turn17view4turn18view0turn18view1turn18view3turn18view4turn18view5

**ACTING-S031 — Trinity Acting for Screen**  
The accessible public source family provides explicit screen-specific evidence. The overview page says every performance task integrates eyeline and camera framing, and it identifies separate screen demands: full-body framing, close-up expression, direct-to-camera ident, dialogue with read-in lines, a listening task built around non-verbal reaction, reflective review, smartphone-or-camera filming and no editing required. The resources page makes the framing pattern even more concrete: wide shot for Ident, close-up for Monologue and Listening, and mid-shot for Dialogue and Playback Review. This is one of the strongest B2 anchors for maintaining a genuine screen-acting layer in later synthesis. The limitation is that the linked guide/syllabus were not equally retrievable in full public text from this run, so later stages should stay within publicly surfaced task language unless a fuller official source is extracted. citeturn21view0turn12view0turn10view0

**ACTING-S032 — Spotlight self-tape essentials**  
This article is professional guidance rather than a formal assessment framework, but it contains high-value observable boundaries. It supports smartphone capture, recommends landscape filming, plain backgrounds, eye-level camera placement, minimal background noise, soft forward lighting, reader use, eyeline kept close to camera, mid-close framing by default, limited props and costume, and restraint on repeated takes. It also says to read instructions carefully and follow task-specific requests, including whether an ident is needed. The source is particularly useful for separating assessability from production polish: the article contains equipment suggestions, but its core guidance is about making the work readable, not about rewarding expensive kit. That should inform later specificity and fairness language, not become hard scoring criteria. citeturn19view0turn19view1turn19view2turn19view3turn19view4

**ACTING-S033 — RADA self-tape guide PDF**  
This is a concise but very useful official setup document. It prioritises one thing above all else: the panel must be able to see and hear the performer clearly. It explicitly says no specialist equipment is needed, phone capture is acceptable, landscape framing is required, a plain background is preferred, quiet rooms improve voice clarity, and costumes/props are unnecessary. It also gives task structure: start wide, then if possible move to a mid shot, state the three pieces being performed and why they were chosen, and perform two classical speeches plus one contemporary speech. It instructs candidates not to look straight down the lens during speeches but to place the eyeline slightly to the side as if speaking to another character. It further discourages excessive retakes and closes with a clear anti-polish message: do not overthink it; they want to see the real person. Because this document is tied to a specific RADA course and includes a song task, later synthesis must use it as a self-tape/text-type anchor rather than as a universal acting-only rulebook. citeturn16view0turn16view1turn16view2turn17view1

**ACTING-S034 — Spotlight performer self-tape guide**  
This source family was correctly completed as process-only. The represented and self-represented performer pages explain how requests are received, where the performer sees instructions, how due dates and statuses are displayed, how to accept or decline a request, and how uploads work. They also note that once a tape is submitted it cannot be edited or deleted, that self-represented performers cannot submit via the app, and which file formats the platform accepts. That makes this source relevant to self-tape workflow and to the distinction between performance guidance and platform admin, but it does not supply acting-form, text-type or performance-criteria language. It should therefore be kept in the handoff as a workflow boundary source, not promoted into scoring language later. citeturn20view0turn20view1turn20view2turn20view3turn20view4turn20view5

## Provisional B2 Findings

**ACTING-B2-F01**  
Formal B2 sources do not treat acting as one generic standard. General acting frameworks and screen-acting frameworks are separated at source level: the acting specifications focus on vocal, physical and characterisation skills across monologue, duologue and staged extracts, while the screen framework adds camera-framing, eyeline, dialogue-with-read-ins, listening and direct-to-camera ident tasks.  
- **Supported by source IDs:** ACTING-S029, ACTING-S030, ACTING-S031  
- **Source type:** Formal assessment framework  
- **Acting forms / context affected:** Stage/general acting, monologue, duologue, screen acting, self-tape  
- **Tape-observable / live-room / both:** Both  
- **Evidence confidence:** High  
- **Why it matters later:** It directly supports later subtype-specific Acting language and blocks a single flattened acting standard.  
- **Caution:** Trinity Screen gives the clearest screen-task structure, but it is still a qualification source rather than a professional casting rubric. citeturn11view0turn21view1turn21view0turn12view0

**ACTING-B2-F02**  
Camera relationship is task-dependent rather than universal. Trinity Screen requires direct address in the Ident task, uses read-in lines for Dialogue, and assigns different frame sizes by task; RADA tells candidates not to look straight down the lens for speeches; Spotlight places eyeline close to camera unless instructions say otherwise.  
- **Supported by source IDs:** ACTING-S031, ACTING-S032, ACTING-S033  
- **Source type:** Formal screen framework; professional self-tape guidance; official conservatoire self-tape guidance  
- **Acting forms / context affected:** Screen acting, self-tape, monologue/speech, dialogue  
- **Tape-observable / live-room / both:** Tape-observable  
- **Evidence confidence:** High  
- **Why it matters later:** Later Acting outputs will need task-aware eyeline wording, not a single blanket rule.  
- **Caution:** Some variation is source-family and task specific; do not convert it into one universal eyeline metric. citeturn21view0turn12view0turn19view1turn16view1

**ACTING-B2-F03**  
B2 gives strong evidence that self-tape assessability should prioritise clear sight and sound, simple framing, readable domestic capture and restraint on props/costume/retakes rather than production polish. Smartphone capture is explicitly acceptable in both Trinity Screen and RADA sources, and Spotlight’s guidance is likewise built around practical readability rather than elaborate production.  
- **Supported by source IDs:** ACTING-S031, ACTING-S032, ACTING-S033  
- **Source type:** Formal screen framework; professional self-tape guidance; official conservatoire self-tape guidance  
- **Acting forms / context affected:** Self-tape across acting forms  
- **Tape-observable / live-room / both:** Tape-observable  
- **Evidence confidence:** High  
- **Why it matters later:** This will be central to later assessability wording and anti-polish guardrails.  
- **Caution:** Spotlight includes gear preferences that should not become scoring rules. citeturn21view0turn19view0turn19view2turn16view0turn16view1turn17view1

**ACTING-B2-F04**  
Voice and speech are clearly treated here as acting evidence rather than singing evidence. Across LAMDA and Trinity acting specifications, the recurring markers are audibility, clarity of diction, vocal control, modulation, pace, pitch, volume and vocal response to text/character.  
- **Supported by source IDs:** ACTING-S029, ACTING-S030  
- **Source type:** Formal assessment framework  
- **Acting forms / context affected:** General acting, monologue, duologue, stage acting  
- **Tape-observable / live-room / both:** Both  
- **Evidence confidence:** High  
- **Why it matters later:** It provides a direct source base for containing the reused `vocal` field to speech/voice-delivery meaning in acting contexts, without drifting into singing language.  
- **Caution:** Some voice-related evidence in these frameworks is coupled with reflection/Q&A rather than raw performance alone. citeturn11view0turn11view2turn9view1turn13view6

**ACTING-B2-F05**  
Context and preparation knowledge are often assessed separately from the performed extract. LAMDA uses examiner questions about character feeling, location, objective, role in the whole play and author/style influences; Trinity uses reflection on material, rehearsal process, style, voice/body techniques and pair collaboration.  
- **Supported by source IDs:** ACTING-S029, ACTING-S030  
- **Source type:** Formal assessment framework  
- **Acting forms / context affected:** General acting, monologue, duologue/pair acting  
- **Tape-observable / live-room / both:** Both  
- **Evidence confidence:** High  
- **Why it matters later:** It strongly supports a later boundary between what a tape shows and what must not be overclaimed unless directly evidenced or separately provided.  
- **Caution:** These are exam structures, not identical to audition structures, but the boundary remains highly relevant. citeturn11view1turn11view2turn9view2turn13view6

**ACTING-B2-F06**  
Text-type and medium boundaries matter, but not identically across sources. Trinity Acting keeps acting extracts tied to theatre and period/language-style contrast, while LAMDA’s acting syllabus permits feature-film material in some acting contexts and sets different restrictions at advanced levels. RADA’s self-tape guide explicitly separates classical and contemporary speeches.  
- **Supported by source IDs:** ACTING-S029, ACTING-S030, ACTING-S033  
- **Source type:** Formal assessment framework; official conservatoire self-tape guidance  
- **Acting forms / context affected:** General acting, stage acting, speech/monologue, text-type handling  
- **Tape-observable / live-room / both:** Both  
- **Evidence confidence:** High  
- **Why it matters later:** Later synthesis must not force one uniform classical/contemporary/stage/screen rule across all Acting subdomains.  
- **Caution:** These are task frameworks, not one universal industry hierarchy of text types. citeturn13view0turn18view1turn18view3turn16view1

## Reusable Handoff Pack for ACTING-B3 and Later ACTING-SYN

**ACTING-B2 handoff summary**

ACTING-B2 has now completed the form-specific extraction layer that B1 was missing. The strongest gain is that there is now a credible public-source basis for treating general acting and screen acting as related but non-identical assessment contexts. The general formal frameworks, especially LAMDA Exams and Trinity Acting, emphasise vocal, physical and characterisation skills, interpretive choices, context and reflection across monologue, duologue and range-of-style work. By contrast, Trinity Acting for Screen adds task-specific screen evidence: eyeline, frame adaptation, direct-to-camera ident work, dialogue with read-in lines, non-verbal listening and filmed playback review. This means later Acting synthesis no longer has to infer a screen layer from admissions logistics alone. citeturn11view0turn21view1turn21view0turn12view0

B2 also materially strengthens the assessability spine. Spotlight’s self-tape essentials and RADA’s self-tape guide both prioritise readable sound, clear framing, plain backgrounds, limited props/costume, manageable domestic capture and restraint on repeated takes. RADA is especially useful because it combines simple-capture guidance with explicit acting text-type asks: two classical speeches and one contemporary speech, framed first wide and then, if possible, at mid shot, with the eyeline placed slightly off lens. Together with Trinity Screen’s task-specific framing, this is enough to support later work on camera relationship, screen-specific observability and anti-polish guardrails without importing production-value bias. citeturn19view0turn19view1turn16view0turn16view1turn21view0turn12view0

A third important gain is semantic clarity around spoken acting voice. Both LAMDA and Trinity repeatedly treat speech evidence as audibility, clarity, diction, modulation, pace, pitch, volume and response to the text or character, not singing. They also make visible a crucial claim-scope boundary: some evidence sits in separate questions or reflections about context, objective, rehearsal and interpretation rather than in the performed extract itself. That will matter later when the Acting branch addresses the reused `vocal` field and the risk of overclaiming from finished tapes. citeturn11view0turn11view1turn11view2turn9view1turn9view2

The remaining B2 limitations are clear. Comedy and physical-theatre criteria remain thin. Publicly exposed stage-versus-screen naturalism language is thinner than task-specific screen framing. Accessibility-specific acting-assessment evidence is not yet complete and must still be picked up in B4. ACTING-S034 should be preserved as process-only workflow context, not promoted into rubric wording. citeturn21view0turn10view0turn20view0turn20view2
