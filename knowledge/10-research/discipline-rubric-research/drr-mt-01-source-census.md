# Musical Theatre Source Census

## Executive summary

This discovery pass establishes a discovery-level source universe for Musical Theatre audition assessment and organises it into four research batches. Across the public web, I located official or high-authority sources spanning UK conservatoires, US Musical Theatre programmes, exam boards, the sector-wide Musical Theatre Common Prescreen material, and casting/practitioner guidance. The strongest discovery cluster is **official admissions/audition guidance**; the strongest explicit-assessment cluster is **exam-board syllabi**. Casting-body material is thinner and more general, with the clearest official UK body source being the **Casting Directors’ Guild** on self-tapes rather than a Musical Theatre-specific rubric. citeturn32search1turn28search5turn29search2

At discovery level, every **mandatory named source** has now been assigned a status: **LAMDA, Trinity College London, Mountview, Bird College, AMDA, Boston Conservatory/BoCo, and the CDG/comparable casting guidance were located**; **RADA was located but appears more relevant here through short-course/audition-training or adjacent material than through a degree-level MT admissions rubric**; **Guildhall remains unresolved for MT-specific public audition criteria in this pass**. That means the mandatory source universe is covered for census purposes even where some items still need full extraction in later batches. citeturn6search3turn28search5turn32search1

The pages already reviewed show that the later analysis stages should expect to capture at least these recurring evidence areas: repertoire era/style splits (for example legit/pre-1965 versus contemporary/post-1965), acting–voice–dance integration, self-tape setup expectations, quick redirection/adaptability, individuality/authenticity, and signs of professional readiness or training potential. Explicit subtype labelling for **Golden Age, contemporary MT, pop/rock, dance-led, actor-musician, and style/genre contrasts** appears in several institutional sources, but **jukebox** and **through-composed** are not commonly surfaced as stand-alone assessment categories on public admissions pages discovered so far. citeturn14search0turn7search2turn21search0turn18search2turn17search2

## Handoff for MT-B1

MT-B1 should be treated as the **core admissions-and-auditions evidence batch**. The job is not yet to recommend rubric changes, but to extract what institutions explicitly ask for, what they explicitly value, and where they imply criteria through structure. Priority institutions are: **LAMDA, Mountview, Bird, ArtsEd, RWCMD, RCS, GSA, Leeds, Urdang, AMDA, BoCo, University of Michigan, CCM, Carnegie Mellon, Penn State, Shenandoah, Pace, NYU Tisch**, with Associated Studios as a useful vocational comparator. citeturn6search3turn7search2turn8search0turn14search0turn23search0turn24search0

For each source, extract in separate fields:

- **Explicit assessment criteria**: any direct statements such as “we value…”, “we are looking for…”, “evaluation includes…”, or identified audition dimensions.  
- **Implied criteria from requirements**: song era/style splits, monologue rules, dance format, self-tape framing/audio requirements, redirection, recall tasks, interview tasks, devised work, wildcard/personal statement requests.  
- **Training potential vs finished product**: any language about raw talent, potential, willingness to take direction, risk-taking, collaboration, or who the programme is for.  
- **Integration markers**: places where acting, singing and movement are explicitly integrated rather than treated as separate silos.  
- **Subtype/style markers**: legit/pre-1965, contemporary/post-1965, pop/rock, actor-musician, dance-led, or any other style distinctions.  
- **Professional-readiness markers**: audition technique, repertoire development, showcase/career prep, industry alignment, or collaboration norms.

Tag every finding as one of: **explicit criterion**, **implied criterion**, **pedagogic framing**, or **process requirement**. Preserve exact wording where concise, but avoid premature synthesis. If a page is mainly marketing copy, mark it as such rather than over-inferring. If a school’s public page does not state what it values, record that absence clearly.
