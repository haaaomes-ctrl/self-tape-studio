## Batch-Level Findings

Recurring exam-board themes are now much clearer than in MT-B1. Across LAMDA, Trinity, LCME and RSL, the repeated assessment pattern is: technical control, interpretative decision-making, contextual knowledge, and communication/audience engagement. Trinity names those areas through technical skills, engagement with material, and audience awareness; LCME uses technical accomplishment, interpretation, knowledge/understanding and communication; RSL splits acting/singing/dancing criteria but still grades performance, technique, and understanding/reflection. citeturn27view8turn12view3turn14view0turn18view1turn28view4turn25view4

The difference from admissions frameworks is substantial. These exam-board sources publish formal learning outcomes, task structures, explicit attainment bands, and component/domain weightings; admissions pages usually publish tasks and institutional values but much less public scoring logic. Trinity, LCME and RSL all state mark bands or percentage bands openly, and several also explain how reflection/discussion contributes to the result. citeturn31view0turn27view9turn27view15turn14view0turn18view1turn25view3

Integration across acting, voice and movement is central, but the sources do not all organise it the same way. LAMDA Solo/Duo and Group are strongly “acting-through-song” frameworks; Trinity is fully integrated and extends into solo, pair, group, and production modes; LCME deliberately separates singer-led and actor-led routes at grade level, then reunifies them in the 2025 diploma; RSL is group-led overall but adds an individual technical specialism at higher grades; Acceptd separates songs, monologues, dance, ballet and wild card into discrete prescreen assets. citeturn26search0turn26search1turn31view0turn15search2turn15search3turn15search0turn25view1turn29view0turn29view1turn29view2

The strongest descriptor language in this batch comes from Trinity, LCME Actors, LCME Diplomas, and RSL. Those sources most clearly describe what Distinction/Merit/Pass look like in terms of audibility, projection, characterisation, contextual knowledge, engagement, movement use, interpretative truth, and reflection quality. citeturn27view10turn27view17turn27view18turn27view19turn28view2turn28view4

## Evidence Gaps Discovered

The batch still leaves some gaps, but they are now narrower and better defined.

First, subtype calibration remains uneven. Acceptd explicitly references pre/post-1970 contrast and examples such as belt versus head/falsetto mix, and LCME Diplomas lists genres such as Golden Age, jukebox and sung-through, but the exam-board sources do not consistently publish fine-grained public descriptors for, for example, legit versus belt, Golden Age acting conventions versus contemporary speech-song, or dance-style-specific thresholds at the level many admissions panels use informally. citeturn29view0turn16view0

Second, professional-readiness and casting-suitability language is much thinner than assessment language. The exam boards measure performance quality, interpretation, contextual understanding, communication, and reflection; they rarely publish anything like “castability”, role fit, or industry readiness as a formal criterion. That contrasts with parts of MT-B1. citeturn31view0turn18view1turn25view4

Third, dance is present but not always equally deep. Trinity integrates movement/dance into overall performance; LAMDA embeds physical response and staging; RSL publishes dance-specific technique points; LCME Diplomas is more holistic and less dance-call-specific than a conservatoire audition. Acceptd is strong on framing and “show your strengths” but has no mark bands. citeturn27view11turn20view1turn24view2turn18view1turn29view2

## Reusable Handoff Pack for MT-B3

### MT-B3 handoff summary

MT-B2 is complete for the visible batch. The strongest contribution of this batch is not more admissions-task detail, but formalised assessment language. LAMDA, Trinity, LCME and RSL all publish learning outcomes, mark bands, grading categories, or component/domain weightings that were largely missing from MT-B1 institutional admissions pages. The most useful sources for later calibration work are Trinity’s graded Musical Theatre syllabus, LCME Musical Theatre for Actors 2024, LCME Musical Theatre Diplomas 2025, and RSL/PAA Musical Theatre. These give the clearest public vocabulary for technical skill, interpretation, contextual understanding, communication, audience awareness, and reflection. citeturn31view0turn14view0turn18view1turn25view4

MT-B2 also clarifies structural distinctions inside the discipline. LAMDA Solo/Duo and Group are strongly acting-through-song frameworks; Trinity is fully integrated across solo, pair, group and production; LCME divides singer-led and actor-led routes at grade level before re-unifying them in the 2025 diploma; RSL is group-led but introduces an individual technical specialism; Acceptd provides a widely used prescreen standard but no adjudication model. citeturn26search0turn26search1turn31view0turn15search2turn15search3turn15search0turn25view1turn29view4

The remaining gap for MT-B3 is therefore practitioner judgement rather than exam-board structure: how casting professionals and high-authority industry bodies talk about self-tapes, recall value, what reads on camera, what makes material feel truthful rather than merely technically accomplished, and how they separate professional suitability from training descriptors. MT-B3 should not repeat exam-board mark bands. It should test where practitioner language agrees with, sharpens, or challenges the formal frameworks found here—especially around individuality, professional readiness, self-tape communication, and submission-level decision making.
