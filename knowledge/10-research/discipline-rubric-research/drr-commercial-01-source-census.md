# Commercial S0 Source Census and Batch Plan

## Scope and method

This census was built to support the Commercial branch only. It does **not** rewrite the rubric, does **not** change the product architecture, and does **not** propose implementation. It identifies a lean, verified source universe for later extraction, with priority given to commercial casting guidance, self-tape guidance, union/professional process rules, and accessibility/fairness safeguards. The strongest public corpus is concentrated in materials from entity["organization","Equity","uk performers union"], entity["organization","SAG-AFTRA","us performers union"], entity["organization","The Casting Directors' Guild","uk ireland casting guild"], entity["organization","Spotlight","casting platform uk"], entity["organization","Casting Networks","casting platform us"] and entity["organization","Backstage","performing arts careers platform"], with lower-priority gap-fill material from entity["organization","London Academy of Music & Dramatic Art","drama school london"], entity["organization","Royal Academy of Dramatic Art","drama school london"] and entity["company","Voices.com","voice marketplace canada"]. citeturn14search2turn19view2turn20view3turn10search2turn18search2turn18search3turn8search15

Two patterns are already clear. First, there is a solid evidence base for **Commercial as a distinct performance context**: realism, tone calibration, camera relationship, copy handling, and simple, legible self-tape execution recur across named casting-director and platform guidance. Second, the strongest official guardrails are **process, fairness and access rules**: clear instructions, realistic timelines, no improper personal questions, no nudity in self-tapes, no paid-reader expectation, and judging performance rather than expensive equipment. citeturn0search1turn6search3turn0search7turn0search14turn0search8turn2view0turn3view1turn19view2turn20view1turn21view0

What is **not** strong at S0 is equally important. Public, high-authority guidance is still thin for UGC/social-style commercial work, presenter-led product video, and fully generalisable corporate/industrial descriptors. Searches in those areas surfaced mostly job-listing patterns or client-facing voiceover material rather than stable, audition-assessment guidance, so those areas should stay in the “gap-fill only / may defer” bucket until extraction confirms otherwise. citeturn13search10turn22search0turn23search20turn13search0turn22search8turn8search15

## Coverage analysis

The **highest-confidence extraction core** is B1 plus B3. B1 gives the clearest answer to the question “what makes Commercial meaningfully different from generic acting?”: multiple sources converge on realism, tone calibration, camera relationship, and direct-address or copy-handling choices rather than theatre-sized performance or vague “presence” language. B3 then places hard limits around what can and cannot be claimed: clear information, realistic self-tape burdens, no improper personal questioning, no nudity in self-tapes, no reader-payment expectation, access-led adjustments, and explicit separation between performance value and expensive setup. citeturn14search2turn6search1turn6search3turn0search7turn0search14turn0search8turn2view0turn19view2turn20view1turn21view0

B2 is also strong, but it is mostly **execution evidence**, not Commercial-specific theory. It is the right batch for assessability rules: framing, reader placement, eyeline, audibility, simple backgrounds, password-protected submissions and avoiding over-produced tapes. Those sources are essential for output-specificity later, but they should not be mistaken for commercial-performance scoring definitions on their own. citeturn15view2turn16view2turn16view3turn8search8turn8search5turn0search2turn14search3turn0search4

B4 is clearly **conditional**. It is useful if later extraction shows a genuine need to model voiceover-style commercial tasks, corporate/explainer copy or screen-training vocabulary, but these sources are thinner, more course-shaped, more client-facing, or more practitioner-shaped than the B1–B3 corpus. They are appropriate for carefully bounded descriptor support, not for primary scoring logic. citeturn18search2turn18search3turn22search8turn22search2turn8search15

One especially important negative finding already emerges from the official codes. None of the higher-authority materials support marketability, bookability, appearance, follower count, studio polish, charm, or class-coded presentation as valid performance evidence. The stronger official sources instead emphasise fair questioning, equal access, clear instructions, and judging performers on performance rather than equipment or irrelevant personal characteristics. citeturn3view1turn19view2turn20view1turn20view0turn16view0

| B4 | COMMERCIAL-S025 to COMMERCIAL-S030 | Fill only genuine gaps | Run only if B1–B3 leave unresolved questions around voiceover-style copy, corporate/explainer context or screen-training language | Avoids over-expanding into low-authority or off-scope material |

Recommended extraction order: **B1 → B3 → B2 → B4 (conditional)**. That order first defines what Commercial is, then fixes what Commercial may not overclaim, then grounds tape-visible execution, and only then fills any surviving gaps.

## Open questions and limitations

The source universe is strong enough to proceed, but several limits should remain explicit before extraction begins.

The first limitation is subtype coverage. Public, higher-confidence guidance is much stronger for **mainstream commercial casting and self-taping** than it is for UGC/social-style work, presenter-led product video, and broad corporate/industrial contexts. Searches in those areas turned up either job boards or client-facing material more often than stable assessment guidance, so those subtypes should not be overbuilt at audit stage unless B4 produces genuine convergence. citeturn13search10turn22search0turn23search20turn13search0turn23search31

The second limitation is source shape. Several useful Commercial-specific articles are still **named-expert or coach-shaped** rather than contractual or standards-based. They are valuable because they name observable behaviours, but they must be triangulated rather than imported directly into rubric language. citeturn6search1turn6search3turn0search8turn22search8

The third limitation is cross-branch risk. Some self-tape and screen-performance guidance is generic enough that it could leak into Acting or Voice if applied carelessly. Later extraction and synthesis will need to keep Commercial-specific camera relationship, copy communication and no-brief brand/product constraints scoped to Commercial only. citeturn15view2turn16view3turn8search8turn22search8
