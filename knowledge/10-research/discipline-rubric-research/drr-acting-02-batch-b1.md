## Source-by-Source Evidence Notes

### ACTING-S001 — RADA BA Acting admissions
RADA’s current BA Acting page sets out a four-stage process: preliminary self-tape, second-round self-tape, short workshop, then full workshop day plus interview. The preliminary self-tape requires one classical speech, one contemporary speech, and a short spoken introduction. The second-round self-tape adds a different classical speech and a short unaccompanied song. RADA states that workshop stages are where the panel looks for imaginative response to text and character, physical and vocal adaptability, ability to take direction, and how the applicant works and observes in a group. The page also contains explicit disabled-applicant support language and equality language. This is strong evidence for later separation of tape-observable material from workshop-only evidence. citeturn26view0turn26view1turn26view4turn26view5

### ACTING-S002 — LAMDA BA Professional Acting
The BA Professional Acting course page mainly provides admissions framing, fees and a route to the audition-process page rather than detailed acting tasks. It is still useful as an official BA acting admissions source and as confirmation that audition structure is handled elsewhere in LAMDA’s admissions system. Treat this source as process / framing only in later synthesis. citeturn27view0

### ACTING-S003 — LAMDA audition process
LAMDA’s audition-process page is one of the strongest B1 sources for official self-tape acting evidence. It requires two contrasting monologues, one from LAMDA’s classical list and one contemporary speech, and states that LAMDA wants to see character choices and interpretation of the text, prepared without outside help or direction. The same page explicitly separates self-tapes from later in-person workshop and final recall stages, and includes inclusive audition-adjustment language. It also states that unsuccessful self-tape links are deleted and that self-tape feedback is not provided. citeturn27view3turn27view5turn27view6turn27view7turn28view0turn28view1turn28view3

### ACTING-S004 — Guildhall drama auditions
Guildhall’s drama-auditions route confirms a self-recorded first round followed by in-person later stages. Through the linked official acting-audition requirements content, Guildhall asks for an introduction and two monologues, one classical and one modern, and asks candidates to explain who they are, what they are performing and why they have chosen the pieces. The linked page also uses language about seeing candidates’ interests in the wider world and the context of the pieces, which is useful later for text/context evidence rather than generic praise. citeturn2view3turn8view0

### ACTING-S005 — Guildhall auditions and interviews
Guildhall’s school-wide auditions/interviews page explicitly says auditions are designed to assess attainment and potential. For Acting it confirms a three-stage process with a self-recorded first round and in-person second round and final recall. It also contains concrete disability-arrangements language, including reasonable adjustments and step-free room access where needed. This is a strong official source for potential-language and accessibility language in admissions. citeturn29view0turn29view2

### ACTING-S008 — Bristol Old Vic Theatre School MFA Professional Acting
BOVTS’s MFA Professional Acting page gives clear admissions evidence. Preliminary auditions are online and require a verse speech from a classical English play, a prose piece from a 20th/21st-century play, and a short unaccompanied song. The panel looks for a high standard of acting, preparation, and ability to take direction. Successful applicants are then called for an in-person recall with the Head of Course. This is a useful official source for classical/modern contrast, direction-response language and the presence of song inside a professional-acting admissions pathway. citeturn34view0turn34view5

### ACTING-S009 — Royal Conservatoire of Scotland BA Acting
RCS provides unusually clear multi-round acting-audition evidence. First round is live in person or live online, not pre-recorded for this cycle. Applicants prepare either one modern speech plus one verse speech, or two modern speeches, each under two minutes, and the panel may redirect one piece. Round two becomes workshop-style, potentially using a short script sent in advance. Final recall asks for a new monologue and includes an interview. The page also includes explicit fair-access / fee-waiver language and disability-support links. citeturn36view0turn36view1turn37view0turn37view1turn37view2turn37view3

### ACTING-S013 — Mountview what to prepare

### ACTING-B1 handoff summary

ACTING-B1 has produced a usable official-admissions evidence base for later Acting synthesis. The strongest coverage is now in multi-stage audition structure, first-round submission requirements, text-type distinctions and the boundary between what schools ask to see on tape or in initial audition and what they reserve for workshops, recalls, callbacks and interviews. Across RADA, LAMDA, Guildhall, RCS, ArtsEd, East 15, Manchester, Oxford, Juilliard, Yale, Carnegie Mellon and USC, the evidence consistently shows that acting admissions are not treated as one generic presentation standard. Instead, schools repeatedly structure auditions through contrasting speeches or monologues, usually with some form of classical / verse / heightened-text and contemporary distinction, and then use later stages to test redirection, group work, unseen text, interviews, songs, movement tasks or wider flexibility.

B1 also now gives a strong official base for acting self-tape assessability. ArtsEd, East 15, Manchester, Oxford, Carnegie Mellon and USC all provide usable evidence on sound, light, framing, eyeline, edit limits, neutral backgrounds, smartphone acceptability and simple capture. That evidence does not yet amount to a complete screen-acting rubric, but it is already enough to show that later Acting outputs must separate technical readability from acting quality and must not reward production polish as talent evidence.

The other major gain is evidence around speech and voice inside acting admissions. Multiple official sources ask for the candidate’s natural voice, own accent or everyday speaking voice, and several sources care about whether the applicant can communicate text clearly and in context. That will matter later because TapeCoach still routes spoken acting voice through the stored vocal field, so later audit and revision will need to contain that semantics risk without turning spoken acting into singing language.

The main unresolved items are narrow and non-blocking.
