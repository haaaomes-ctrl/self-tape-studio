3. Revision Scope and Preservation Rules
This revision covers wording, evidence requirements, claim-scope rules, report guidance, exclusion language and discipline-specific guardrails for Acting inside the current shared TapeCoach architecture.
The baseline text actually revised is the supplied 0B Acting rubric slice, with Acting-specific additions to the 0E guardrail logic. 14. Reusable Handoff Pack for ACTING-FINAL-AUDIT
ACTING-REV handoff summary
The Acting revision is complete at wording and guardrail level. It does not alter the shared six-field architecture, Acting weights, caps, blockers, verdict thresholds, report schema, backend pipeline or Musical Theatre stabilised flow. The revision strengthens the current Acting baseline by making Acting form-specific inside the existing framework, while keeping all score fields intact.
The most important change is containment of the spoken voice / vocal proxy risk. The revised baseline now states that, although the stored vocal field may continue to operate internally as the speech-delivery slot for Acting, spoken-only reports must not present that category as singing or vocal performance unless singing is actually present. Speech delivery is now framed as audibility, clarity, diction, articulation, modulation, pace, volume and response to text or task, with poor recording quality separated from weak speech performance.
The second major gain is Acting-form specificity. The revised baseline now distinguishes monologue, scene/duologue, screen/self-tape, stage, classical/heightened text, contemporary text and mixed acting tasks. Screen/self-tape guidance now treats camera relationship and eyeline as task-specific rather than universal. Reader handling is now clearly bounded as process support, not a talent prerequisite.
The third major gain is claim-scope control. Response to direction, redirection, group work, workshop behaviour, training potential and broader flexibility are now treated as live-room, admissions-specific or process-only evidence unless directly shown. The no-brief guardrail is also strengthened so role, world, production style, hidden objective and casting-fit claims must be grounded in supplied material or observable evidence.
The fourth major gain is fairness. The revision adds explicit accessibility-safe Acting and speech rules: access needs, visual impairment, neurodivergence, speech difference, mobility difference, support workers, accessible scripts, extra preparation time and adaptation must not be treated as acting deficits. professional_presentation is now bounded to assessability, brief response and safe process, not production polish, paid readers, marketability, appearance or class-coded presentation.
Residual limitations remain but do not block final audit: frontend labels are unverified, no live Acting outputs were supplied, comedy remains thin, physical/fight evidence is specialist-course-shaped, methodology-neutral descriptor language remains limited, and some access-specific operational detail remains broad.
