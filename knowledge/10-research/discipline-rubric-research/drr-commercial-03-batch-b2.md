Across the B2 corpus, the most stable common denominator is legibility rather than polish: a readable frame, a workable eyeline, an off-camera reader where dialogue is involved, clear sound, simple light, and a non-distracting background recur much more consistently than any requirement for expensive gear or studio finish. Where sources become more specific about kit, editing or wardrobe, the advice usually shifts from assessability into platform or practitioner preference, and must therefore be bounded later. citeturn12view1turn4view3turn5view1turn7view1turn8view0turn9view0turn9view8

**Source-by-Source Evidence Notes**

**COMMERCIAL-S009 — Advice for Self-Taping**

- The source treats self-tape execution as a legibility problem: quiet room, simple background, limited props, shadow-aware lighting, stable camera, off-camera reader, landscape phone capture and readable sound. It also gives a specific capture pattern of long shot into close framing. citeturn3view0turn11view0turn12view1
- Framing is explicit: begin with a long shot showing the full body, then move to a close-up just below the shoulders, keep the frame tight through the take, avoid multiple angles and profile-heavy shooting, and do not show the reader. citeturn11view0turn12view1turn3view1
- Eyeline is explicit and non-universal: the performer's focus should stay with a reader positioned next to the camera rather than directly into camera; that clearly supports off-camera reader-style setup, but it must not be turned into a universal Commercial rule because B1 already established that direct-to-camera commercials exist. citeturn11view0turn12view1
- Reader guidance is strong: self-reading, leaving pauses for “imagined” dialogue and reader-app playback are discouraged because they flatten reality; any live reader is preferred. citeturn3view0turn11view0
- Tape-observable evidence includes framing, reader visibility, movement, background, sound quality, hair covering the face, front-vs-overhead lighting, logos / stripes and over-literal costume use. Process-only items include file format, password protection, public-vs-private upload, and slate content. citeturn3view0turn3view2turn12view0turn12view1
- Non-universal or later-bounded items include the English-only slate plus nationality / passport prompts, the “make it look professional” phrase, and the warning about small iPhone files for US productions. Those are not valid later scoring criteria. citeturn11view0turn12view1

**COMMERCIAL-S010 — How to Self-Tape**

- The source gives a straightforward self-tape setup recipe centred on readability: soft lighting, minimal background noise, camera at eye level, front light, plain or grey background, reader near the camera, mid-close framing and clear clip labelling. citeturn4view0turn4view3
- Framing is explicit but not absolute: the default is a mid-close shot from chest to top of head unless the request says otherwise. That makes the source useful for default readability, not for a universal Commercial frame law. citeturn4view3
- Eyeline is explicit: it should sit close to the camera so both sides of the face remain visible; direct camera address is not presented as the default performance rule here. citeturn4view3
- Audio and reader guidance are pragmatic: keep background noise down, sit close to the phone if filming on it, and keep the reader from overrunning the tape vocally. citeturn4view0turn4view3
- Slate, ident, file conversion, naming and sending are well covered, but those remain process-only rather than talent evidence. The same applies to the article’s brand, model and price suggestions. citeturn4view0turn4view3
- The article’s “nothing fancy is needed” line is useful, but it sits alongside optional spend recommendations, so the source should support assessability guardrails rather than equipment-based merit. citeturn4view0

**COMMERCIAL-S011 — Casting Directors’ Self-Tape Top Tips**

- This source is contributor-led and gives the clearest “basic readability over polish” message in the Spotlight subset: the casting team wants to see and hear the performer clearly, in a quiet space, with a readable point of focus and with sensible limits on takes. citeturn5view0turn5view1
- Framing guidance is mostly head-and-shoulders, with ident / longshot only if requested. That is useful as a default, but plainly conditional on instructions. citeturn5view1turn5view4
- Eyeline guidance is mixed and therefore cannot be universalised: one contributor says left / right of camera and not down lens, while another article passage pairs “tight eyeline” with wording that reads as profile-facing. That inconsistency itself is a caution flag. citeturn5view0turn5view1turn5view4
- Reader guidance is practical rather than stylised: the reader should be someone the actor can work freely with, not someone who corrects or blocks them. The source also treats repeated rehearsal and just a few recorded takes as preferable to endless re-taping. citeturn5view4turn5view0
- Tape-observable items are clear visibility, audibility, quiet environment, shot tightness and eye-line control. Process-only elements are instructions, labelling and delivery route. citeturn5view0turn5view6
- The main limitation is contextual: this is a young-performer / competition article whose casting contributors have also cast commercials, but the page is not a Commercial-specific standards document. citeturn2view2

**COMMERCIAL-S012 — A Beginner’s Guide to Self Tapes**

- The source is broad and actor-facing. It is useful for room sufficiency, backdrop flexibility, smartphone acceptability, reader function, close sightlines, slate basics and low-budget workarounds such as books for camera height and borrowed / shared resources. citeturn6view0turn6view1turn6view5turn6view6
- Framing evidence is broader than some of the other sources: it assumes many self-tapes need enough room for a wider or full-body shot, which is important for task-sufficient visibility in some Commercial contexts. citeturn6view0
- Eyeline guidance is explicit but general: sightlines should stay close to camera and accidental looks straight down the lens should be avoided. Reader quality matters because a loud, stiff or stumbling reader becomes a distraction rather than support. citeturn6view1turn6view5
- Audio is treated as make-or-break for assessability, but the article drifts into software-and-polish language by promoting editing and audio tools. That material is useful only as optional process support, not as talent evidence. citeturn6view2turn6view3
- Slate guidance is partially useful and partially risky: most requested slate content should come from the call, extras should not be added, and the source suggests a subtly brighter slate for commercials. That last point is clearly coach-shaped and should not become a scoring norm. citeturn6view5
- Accessibility and communication-support content is thin. The only directly relevant operational note is that Zoom can be workable if no good local reader is available. citeturn6view1

**COMMERCIAL-S013 — Master Your Self Tape: 14 Top Tips From Casting Directors**

- The strongest B2 value here is anti-overproduction language combined with a casting-director roundup: keep the background clean, use clear sound and video, focus on the performance rather than flashy edits, use a live reader and keep the take count low. citeturn7view1turn7view3turn8view0
- The source contributes little on exact shot composition or eyeline, but it helps separate performance quality from packaging. It repeatedly frames framing, wardrobe and file naming as instruction-following tasks rather than aesthetic merit. citeturn7view3turn8view0
- Reader guidance is strong and relevant: do not use a recording of yourself for the other part if a live reader can be used, because a live reader makes the interaction more natural and connected. citeturn7view0turn7view6
- The article is also unusually explicit that casting wants only a few strong takes, not many average ones. That is useful for anti-overproduction and for distinguishing “more coverage” from “better performance.” citeturn7view4
- Process-only or risky material includes submit-early rhetoric, first-impression rhetoric, explicit agent-communication rules, three-point-lighting language and external-mic suggestions. None of that should become talent scoring. citeturn7view1turn7view2turn8view0
- The limitation is source shape: it is a recent platform roundup, not a standard, and it mixes good anti-polish guidance with obvious visibility / professionalism heuristics. citeturn2view4turn8view0

**COMMERCIAL-S014 — How to Make a Perfect Self-Tape**

- This is the broadest and most detailed source in the batch. It is strongest on the distinction between readable technical quality and “professional-level” polish, on reader placement, on medium close-up default framing and on conditional slating. citeturn9view0turn9view1turn9view5turn10view2
- Framing guidance is not one-size-fits-all: the article defaults to a medium close-up for most reads, but also gives a clear example of a tape that was too tight to show how the actor lived in the body, and it recognises that some slates or requests want full-body material. That supports task-sufficient visibility rather than a single frame law. citeturn8view1turn10view2turn13view7
- Reader guidance is detailed: a live reader is preferable, the reader should not distract, reader lines must remain audible, reader accents can distract, and the most effective eyeline is with the reader almost straddling the tripod next to the camera. citeturn9view1
- The source strongly supports neutral or improvised home backgrounds and readable lighting, while also pushing optional gear. The “not necessarily professional-level quality” phrase is important because it limits how far later technical evaluation should drift toward production value. citeturn9view0turn10view0turn13view0turn13view1turn13view4
- Slate, editing and submission are especially process-forward here: slate only if instructed, slate separately, follow directions precisely, and submit electronically. Early submission may sometimes attract redirects, but that is clearly a process fact, not a finished-tape attribute. citeturn10view2turn10view3turn13view2turn13view3turn13view6
- The source’s largest limitation is scope and style: it mixes useful assessability guidance with gear shopping, flattering-colour advice, flattering-neckline advice and other packaging cues that must not become Commercial evidence. citeturn9view1turn10view0turn13view0
