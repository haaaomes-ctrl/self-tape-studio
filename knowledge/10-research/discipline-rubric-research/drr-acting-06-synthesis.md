**Key Acting Assessment Frameworks Found**

The Acting branch now rests on seven distinct evidence families.

- **Official school/conservatoire audition structure** provides the strongest evidence that acting auditions are not one generic task. It shows repeated monologue/speech requirements, classical/contemporary distinctions, self-tape asks, and a clean boundary between initial tape evidence and later recall/workshop/interview evidence. Principal anchors: S001–S005, S009, S013–S020, S022–S027.
- **Formal acting descriptor frameworks** supply the clearest positive acting domains: vocal/speech control, physical expressivity, characterisation, interpretive choices, contextual understanding, and reflection. Principal anchors: S029 and S030.
- **Formal screen-acting task evidence** makes screen acting a related but non-identical context, especially through eyeline, frame adaptation, direct-to-camera tasking, dialogue with read-in lines, and non-verbal listening. Principal anchor: S031.
- **Self-tape assessability and anti-polish guidance** shows what a tape needs in order to be readable and fair: clear sight/sound, manageable domestic capture, limited props/costume, and no requirement for expensive polish. Principal anchors: S032 and S033, with B1 schools as parallel support.
- **Professional/union fairness and workload guidance** prevents self-tape burden being mistaken for acting ability. It adds page limits, proportional turnaround, reader burden rules, privacy, nudity/stunt boundaries and remote-audition fairness. Principal anchors: S035 and S036.
- **Accessibility and anti-deficit evidence** reframes acting assessment through barrier removal rather than performer fault. Principal anchors: S038–S041.
- **Physical / embodied / fight-performance gap-fill evidence** exists, but only through specialist, course-shaped sources, so it supports cautious extension rather than universal standards. Principal anchors: S042 and S043.

**Accessibility, Fairness and Anti-Bias Findings**

Acting synthesis now supports the following high-confidence access and fairness propositions.

- **Access is a barrier-removal issue, not an impairment-failure issue.** The strongest support comes from ScreenSkills and Graeae. This means later Acting outputs must separate performer support needs from acting quality. Source family: S038–S040.
- **Reasonable adjustments extend beyond mobility.** They can include material format, timing, sound, lighting, personal space, communication format, interpreters, support workers, access riders/passports and confidential handling of access information. Source family: S038, S039, S041.
- **Visual-impairment-specific audition handling is directly evidenced.** Editable scripts, advance text, line-feeding, clear verbal instruction, room/set orientation and descriptive direction are not extras; they are fair-process conditions. Source: S041.
- **Access supports and adaptations are process supports, not talent criteria.** This is the single most important anti-deficit boundary for later Acting audit and revision. Source family: S038–S041, supported by S035.
- **Incidental casting materially strengthens anti-bias handling.** It reduces the risk that disability or access need becomes an invented role-defining trait. Source: S038 and S041.
- **Access information must be handled confidentially.** This is an access-process obligation, not performer self-exposure. Source family: S038–S041.
- **Assessability, process adaptation, performance quality and role fit must remain separate concepts.** The sources do not support collapsing them into one judgement.

**Acting synthesis handoff**

The Acting branch now has a usable synthesis base covering 43 source rows across admissions, formal frameworks, self-tape/screen guidance, professional/union process sources, accessibility/inclusion sources and specialist gap-fill sources. The strongest cross-source conclusion is that Acting cannot be treated as one undifferentiated standard. Official admissions sources show repeated separation between tape evidence and live-room evidence, repeated use of monologue/speech structures, and repeated classical/contemporary distinctions. Formal frameworks add the best positive descriptor language for text handling, speech, physicality, characterisation and interpretation, while Trinity’s screen framework adds task-specific evidence for eyeline, frame adaptation, direct address, read-in-line dialogue and non-verbal listening. Self-tape guidance then strengthens assessability: readable sight/sound, simple capture, limited props/costume and anti-polish boundaries. Professional and union sources prevent mediated-tape burden from being misread as talent, adding page limits, turnaround, reader burden, privacy and no-nudity/no-stunt boundaries. Accessibility sources complete the non-deficit access spine: barrier removal, accessible materials, access riders/passports, confidential access handling, incidental casting and visual-impairment-specific adjustments. The main synthesis limitations remain explicit rather than hidden. Comedy is still thin. Rich methodology-neutral positive descriptor language remains thinner than ideal. Neurodivergence-specific acting-assessment detail is broader than it is operational. Positive acting-speech descriptors under access conditions remain stronger at process level than at descriptor level. Anti-bias casting language is materially improved but still thinner than a dedicated bias framework. Physical/embodied/fight acting is now partially mapped, but largely through specialist course-shaped sources rather than universal standards.

**Acting-form / medium / text-type criteria summary**

Monologue, scene/duologue, screen/self-tape, stage, classical/heigh­tened text and contemporary text now have usable differentiated evidence. Comedy remains thin. Physical/embodied and fight-based acting are partially covered only through specialist-course sources. Spoken voice/speech delivery is well supported as acting evidence, not singing.

**Tape-observable versus live-room-only summary**

Tape can reliably show text handling, speech clarity, camera relationship, framing awareness, visible physical choices and some reader-mediated response. Tape cannot safely prove response to direction, workshop behaviour, group work, training potential or access logistics unless explicitly provided in the evidence context.

**Self-tape / video assessability summary**

Readability depends on clear sight/sound, usable framing, simple domestic capture, limited props/costume and task-appropriate camera rules. Production polish, expensive equipment, elaborate sets and paid readers must not become proxies for acting quality.

**Accessibility / fairness summary**

Access needs are process supports, not talent deficits. Reasonable adjustments, accessible materials, extra preparation time, access riders/passports, interpreters, support workers, incidental casting and visual-impairment-specific adjustments are now explicitly evidenced.

**Generic-feedback risk list**

Grounded, natural, believable, connected, strong choices, clear relationship, good pacing, emotionally truthful, strong presence, clear character, good voice and professional self-tape are all unsafe unless tied to named observable evidence.

**Exclusion / anti-bias list**

Exclude singing language for spoken-only acting, appearance, body type, “look”, marketability, fame, follower count, class-coded polish, accent hierarchy, protected characteristics, disability/access needs as deficit, production polish as acting quality, reader availability as talent evidence, method jargon as universal scoring, inferred response to direction, and invented role/world claims without brief support.

**Evidence gaps**

Comedy; richer methodology-neutral positive acting descriptors; neurodivergence-specific acting-assessment detail; positive descriptor language for speech under access conditions; fuller anti-bias casting framework language; direct stage-versus-screen naturalism comparison; broader physical/fight evidence outside specialist-school sources; and the unresolved current Royal Central admissions source.

**Audit priorities**

Later audit should prioritise subtype specificity, spoken-speech versus singing semantics, anti-generic wording, tape-versus-live-room boundaries, production-value exclusion, access-safe non-deficit handling, no-brief claim restraint, and methodology-neutrality.
