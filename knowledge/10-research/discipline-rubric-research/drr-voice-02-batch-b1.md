## Handoff for VOICE-B2

**Reusable Handoff Pack for VOICE-B2**

VOICE-B1 extracted all 20 assigned official / quasi-official source rows and closed every row with a final status. Seventeen rows are official admissions or programme pages from UK and US conservatoires, drama schools, universities, youth choirs or youth MT providers; one row is an official cross-institution framework source (MT Common Prescreen / Acceptd); one row is an official process-only recorded-audition guidance page; and one row is a minor-programme audition page with limited subtype detail. No source row remains unresolved.

The clearest B1 pattern is that contrast is real but not uniform. Official sources repeatedly ask for contrasting songs, but the contrast varies by institution: pre-/post-1965, pre-/post-1970, legit vs contemporary, canon vs contemporary, different voice qualities, or MT plus another genre. This supports a true style/subtype map for Voice / Singing, but not a single universal admissions rule. The strongest recurring subtype anchors are classical/legit, contemporary MT, classical voice / art song, actor-musician, pop-commercial crossover, jazz-standard context, and unaccompanied song.

The second major pattern is that song assessment is not just technique display. Multiple official sources explicitly ask candidates to act through songs, tell a story, show emotional arc, clarify objective/addressee, or reveal musical intelligence. This is the strongest B1 support for keeping acting/storytelling visible inside Song without turning song reports into acting-scene reports.

The third major pattern is assessability-first capture guidance. RAM, RWCMD, RCS, USC, NYU, MTCP and Chapman all stress clear sound, balanced accompaniment, eye-level framing, simple or low-cost setups, and acceptable phone capture. Several sources explicitly reduce production-polish pressure. This is strong evidence that audio and technical clarity are preconditions for fair singing assessment, not vocal merit in themselves.

B1 also sharply clarifies live-room boundaries. Recalls, redirection, learning a new song, workshop collaboration, interviews, musicianship tasks and training-potential judgments are repeatedly located in live or process contexts rather than in submitted song tape alone. This should later feed claim-scope rules and output-specificity tests.

Main deferred gaps for VOICE-B2 are descriptor language: pitch, tone, phrasing, diction, breath/support, musical interpretation, registration, and more detailed belt/mix/pop-rock/jazz/folk language. VOICE-B4 must later handle vocal-health safety, accessibility, speech difference, neurodivergence and gender-diverse voice considerations. Exact source IDs used in B1: VOICE-S001–VOICE-S020. Relevant provisional findings: VOICE-B1-F01–F05.

**Recommended focus for VOICE-B2 Formal Frameworks / Descriptor Extraction**

- Formal descriptor sources for pitch, rhythm, tone, phrasing, diction, musical communication and performance interpretation.
- Carefully bounded legit / belt / mix / classical / CCM terminology.
- Descriptor vocabulary that can improve report specificity without importing external grades, marks or weights.
- Continued separation of tape-observable evidence from process-only musicianship, coaching or health claims.
