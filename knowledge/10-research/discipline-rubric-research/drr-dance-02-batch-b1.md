### Batch-Level Findings

Official B1 admissions sources strongly favour **class-based and workshop-based assessment** over purely repertory-led judging. Ballet schools repeatedly use preliminary class/audition stages, often followed by finals or callbacks, while contemporary-led programmes more often use admissions workshops, improvisation, dialogue and interview. citeturn6view0turn10view12turn8view0turn16search1turn16search0turn14search1turn4view0

The most repeated B1 audition components are: ballet and/or contemporary classwork, solo presentation, improvisation or creative task, interview or reflective conversation, and in several schools a two-stage process that separates initial video or preliminary screening from later live assessment. This is particularly clear at Rambert, ENBS, Central, LCDS, Juilliard and USC Kaufman. citeturn6view0turn10view13turn19view0turn19view1turn16search1turn16search0turn14search1turn4view0

B1 provides useful but uneven style coverage. Ballet is the strongest area, supported by Royal Ballet School, ENBS, Central, Elmhurst and Rambert; contemporary is also well covered through NSCD, Trinity Laban and LCDS. Jazz/commercial evidence is thinner in official admissions sources, but London Studio Centre and Urdang give the clearest public signals, and USC Kaufman adds hip-hop/freestyle at live-final stage. Tap appears mainly as an allowed solo genre at LSC video stage rather than a fully described assessment framework. citeturn10view14turn10view12turn19view0turn10view6turn6view0turn18search0turn8view0turn16search1turn11view2turn15search1turn4view0

## Reusable Handoff Pack

### DANCE-B1 handoff summary

DANCE-B1 is complete at extraction level and is strongest where schools publish either an official audition pack/policy PDF or a linked criteria document. The highest-confidence B1 evidence comes from Rambert, NSCD, ENBS, Central School of Ballet, Bird College, London Studio Centre, USC Kaufman and, to a slightly lesser extent, Trinity Laban and LCDS. Across these sources, the recurring admissions model is: preliminary screening through classwork or video, followed by a more detailed live stage involving technique classes, solo work, improvisation/creative tasks and often interview. Ballet schools lean heavily on class-based observation, pointe/technique splits and potential for professional training. Contemporary-led schools lean more strongly into workshop tasks, improvisation, creative response, dialogue and wider readiness for a portfolio career.

The most important B1 non-regression evidence for later Dance work is about **assessability**. Central and USC Kaufman explicitly require full-body visibility, stable framing, minimal editing and enough proximity for technique and musicality to be seen. London Studio Centre gives the clearest style-specific video task breakdown across ballet, contemporary and jazz/commercial. Urdang confirms first-round auditions by video with contrasting dance solos. At the same time, several sources reserve parts of assessment for live interaction only: interviews, responsiveness to direction, physio checks, physical examinations, seminar-style discussion or workshop adaptation.

Fairness and accessibility are also present in B1. NSCD, Bird, Rambert and LCDS all publish support/adjustment or non-discrimination language tied directly to admissions. This is important because later Dance audit/revision work will need to distinguish legitimate assessability issues from unlawful or unsafe inference about disability, injury history, body presentation or production value.
