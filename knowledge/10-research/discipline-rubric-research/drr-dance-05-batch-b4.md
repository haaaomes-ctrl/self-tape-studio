## Batch-level findings and remaining gaps

**8. Batch-Level Findings**

**Commercial / street / hip-hop evidence outside competition.**  
B4 materially improved this gap. Royal Caribbean now provides current official employer evidence for contemporary, jazz, theatre dance, showgirl and hip-hop in the same professional casting process, with separate hip-hop emphasis on breaking, popping/locking, improv and skills. NCLH strengthens the professional non-competition picture by naming commercial jazz, ballet, contemporary and hip-hop as major training emphases, and Celebrity adds a professional online reel requirement spanning jazz, commercial, contemporary and partnering. Together these sources push commercial/street/hip-hop evidence beyond the competition-heavy B2 set, though they remain employer-shaped rather than universal frameworks. citeturn25view0turn25view1turn25view2

**Professional self-tape / dance-video assessability.**  
B4 is strong here. Candoco explicitly says stage-one video is not about production values and may be smartphone footage. Royal Caribbean says virtual submission videos need not be professionally produced if they are clear. Equity’s general and commercials self-tape guidance both reject home-set transformation, and the commercials code expressly says performers should be judged on performance rather than equipment quality. Celebrity shows what content a professional dance reel is expected to contain, and Candoco adds useful specificity around technique lineage, improvisational style and range of movement. citeturn14view0turn20view0turn23view4turn25view0turn25view1

**Accessibility-safe movement assessment and non-penalisation.**  
This is the strongest B4 addition. Candoco and Stopgap publish concrete dance-specific access arrangements. Equity goes further by treating access as part of the whole creative process: accessible formats, advance materials, accessible technique classes, detailed plans, fatigue-aware scheduling, open movement language, adaptation of material to body and pace of learning, and a ban on medical-history curiosity unless safety requires disclosure. B4 therefore materially stabilises later non-penalisation work. citeturn14view0turn6view2turn19view0turn19view2turn19view4

**Production-value versus assessability.**  
B4 gives unusually clear language here: Candoco says “not about production values”; Royal Caribbean says “not professionally produced” is fine if the material is clear; Equity’s commercial code says performance, not equipment, is what should be judged. This is high-value non-regression evidence against rewarding expensive camera polish. citeturn14view0turn25view0turn23view4

**Choreography pick-up / learning-speed and live-room boundaries.**  
B4 does not create a clean universal descriptor, but it clarifies the split. Royal Caribbean’s virtual route uses learned filmed combinations; Princess and NCLH explicitly rely on live combination learning; Stopgap uses set material plus improvisation and creative tasking. The result is enough evidence to distinguish tape-observable preparation from live-room responsiveness and pickup. citeturn25view0turn25view2turn25view3turn6view2

**Stamina / endurance overclaim boundaries.**  
B4 improves caution more than it creates positive scoring language. Equity explicitly says long or multi-day auditions can be barriers for dancers with fatigue and chronic pain. Candoco explicitly treats energy-limited impairment as an access conversation rather than a reason not to apply. This is useful evidence against overclaiming endurance from short clips or rigid room norms, but it remains a caution field rather than a rich descriptor base. citeturn19view4turn14view0

**Source-type limitations.**  
B4’s commercial/professional evidence is strongest in cruise and touring-entertainment employers. These pages often mix valuable style/process evidence with company-specific role constraints such as heights, silhouettes, attire and ship-travel requirements. Those constraints are important provenance context but must not be turned into general dance scoring criteria. citeturn25view0turn25view1turn25view2turn25view3

Body- or height-specific casting filters, “lean silhouette” language, swimsuit/photo requests, ship-travel logistics, role-specific heel demands, nudity/commercial administration rules, and any employer-specific aesthetic preference must remain source-specific context only. citeturn25view0turn25view1turn23view4
