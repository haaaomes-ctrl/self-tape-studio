# Discipline Rubric Research — programme overview

> **Thread root.** This note narrates a five-discipline rubric-research programme run **3–12 May 2026**, imported into the corpus on 2026-06-08 from the folder `Research/Rubric Research Each Discipline`. The thread is **68 stage notes across five discipline chains**, plus five per-discipline overviews and one open-question gap note. It is **research evidence, not the live rubric** — the consolidated rubric for each discipline lives in that discipline's archived *Merged* file under `knowledge/90-archive/discipline-rubric-research/`, referenced from each discipline overview.

## Summary

Five performance disciplines were each taken through the same research pipeline to produce discipline-specific assessment rubrics **inside TapeCoach's existing shared six-field scoring schema**, without destabilising the protected Musical Theatre acting-plus-song flow. The disciplines were done sequentially and the method matured as it went:

- **[[drr-mt-overview]]** — Musical Theatre (the pioneer and protected anchor)
- **[[drr-dance-overview]]** — Dance (weakest prior credibility; proved architecture-aware revision works)
- **[[drr-acting-overview]]** — Acting (the `vocal`-as-speech overload; anti-generic feedback)
- **[[drr-voice-overview]]** — Voice / singing voice (disambiguated the overloaded `vocal` field at the rule layer)
- **[[drr-commercial-overview]]** — Commercial (the most mature method; added an implementation-readiness stage)

Open question carried forward: **[[drr-voice-commercial-ratification-gap]]**.

## The method (one pipeline, five times)

Each discipline ran: **source census (S0) → four targeted extraction batches (B1–B4) → synthesis → gap audit → revision → final audit → output-specificity spec → QA → V5 maturity audit → lessons → cross-branch handoff**, then a later consolidation into a single *Merged* document (12 May). The shape is visible in every chain; the deviations below are where a discipline departed from it.

## How the research expanded

The programme's throughline is that rubric quality did **not** come from inventing new scoring theory. It came from making the existing shared schema more truthful about what it can and cannot judge, anchoring every claim to observable tape evidence, and converting that into testable output behaviour. MT established the pipeline and the discipline of an unbroken traceability chain (source → finding → audit recommendation → revision → final-audit check) under a hard "protected anchor" constraint. Dance proved the same method works for a discipline that previously had *no* true rubric and was proxy-mapped onto the `vocal` field. Acting confronted the same `vocal`-field overload from the speech-delivery side and made anti-generic feedback the acceptance test. Voice resolved the overload explicitly at the rule layer (the `vocal` field = sung-vocal evidence only in Song/MT). Commercial, the most mature pass, added an implementation-readiness stage that translates the rubric into a code-facing requirement pack.

### Recurring cross-cutting findings

- **The shared `vocal` field is overloaded** across singing, speech-delivery and dance-technique. This is the single most important architectural finding of the whole programme; it was mitigated at the wording/rule layer in every discipline, never by renaming the field or changing the schema.
- **"Wording is not done until it is testable output behaviour."** Each discipline's acceptance standard became output-specificity rules + non-regression tests, not improved prose.
- **QA across every discipline was synthetic/adversarial** — no live discipline-specific reports, PDFs or comparison pages existed. Live-output QA is the standing gate that blocks production sign-off; consolidation is not blocked.
- **Musical Theatre acting-plus-song is the protected non-regression anchor** named throughout.

## Deviations recorded as findings (per the agreed ordering decision)

1. **Commercial late-stage order (corrected).** The Commercial Output-Spec consumes the Final-Audit (cites D01/D02), so the chain follows that content dependency — **Final-Audit → Output-Spec** — overriding the misleading file-date order (the Output-Spec file is timestamped earlier). Commercial also carries a **unique implementation-readiness step** ([[drr-commercial-11-implementation]]) that no other discipline has — production requirements + QA pack, **not** live wiring.
2. **Commercial B2/B3 birthtime inversion** — `C-B2` was created after `C-B3` by clock; ordered by batch number (B2 before B3).
3. **Voice has no "Lessons" step** — its chain runs straight from output-spec → V5 maturity audit → handoff.
4. **Voice taxonomy.** Voice is filed under `discipline: commercial` with a `voice` sub-area tag as an **operator taxonomy decision**. The source research does *not* fold Voice under Commercial: it treats "Voice" as the **sung / singing voice** (Song/MT reference) and asserts only a *label-boundary* to Commercial (song semantics must not leak into Commercial's naturalism; "commercial" must never mean marketability). Carried as an open question for expert ratification — see [[drr-voice-commercial-ratification-gap]].
5. **Two Acting files are mislabelled vs content** — `A-Lessons Learned.md` ([[drr-acting-12-lessons]]) actually carries a next-discipline recommendation, and `A-Syn-OUTPUT-SPEC.md` ([[drr-acting-10-output-spec]]) is a generic handoff template; the real Acting output-spec lives in §9 of the Acting Final Handoff ([[drr-acting-14-final-handoff]]). Titles kept stage-based; content preserved verbatim.
