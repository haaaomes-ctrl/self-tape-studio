## Proposed Batch Plan

**DANCE-B1** covers official schools, conservatoires and admissions and is intentionally the largest batch at **36 sources**. These are the strongest public sources for what schools actually ask dancers to do, show or submit, and they are the richest evidence base for tape-observable criteria, admissions workshops, video routes and training-potential language.

**DANCE-B2** covers exam boards, syllabi, adjudication and formal assessment frameworks at **18 sources**. This batch is where technique language, progression structures, explicit criteria, reasonable-adjustment boundaries and competition evaluation descriptors are most likely to be extractable in a disciplined way.

**DANCE-B3** covers commercial, theatre, practitioner and professional guidance at **9 sources**. It is smaller because public high-authority sources here are thinner and more heterogeneous. These sources are valuable for professional expectations and language, but they must not automatically be turned into scoring criteria.

**DANCE-B4** covers technique, accessibility, self-tape and gap-fill sources at **9 sources**. This batch is reserved for sources that are crucial for fairness, non-penalisation, assessability and filmed-dance evidence, especially where formal admissions pages do not say enough about framing, accessible practice or safe dance space.

High-priority placement decisions: admissions-embedded video pages such as Central, London Studio Centre, USC Kaufman and Urdang were kept in **B1** because they are part of official entry criteria, even though they also inform self-tape practice. By contrast, **Arden**, **Mountview** and **GSA** were kept in **B4** because they are most useful as generic or transferable filmed-audition guidance rather than core dance-admissions frameworks. **RAD** and **ISTD** accessibility-adjustment documents were kept in **B2**, not B4, because they are attached to formal assessment systems and later extraction will need to preserve the distinction between changing process and changing criteria. **Rambert** appears in both school and company form: the **Rambert School** admissions sources remain in B1, while the **Rambert company auditions** page is placed in B3 because it reflects professional hiring rather than training admissions. citeturn18search0turn7search15turn13search7turn16search4turn21search1

## Early Evidence-Gap Watchlist

The most important watchpoint for extraction is the gap between **strong ballet/contemporary admissions evidence** and **thinner formal public criteria for jazz, tap, commercial/street and hip-hop**. Those latter styles are represented, but more often via competition rules, mixed vocational programmes or practitioner guidance than via standalone conservatoire rubrics.

A second watchpoint is **tape-observable versus live-room-only evidence**. Several sources make it clear that schools and companies use workshops, interviews, improvisation, callbacks or class learning to assess things that a static self-tape may not fully show. Later extraction must separate what can genuinely be inferred from a recorded dance submission from what belongs to a live audition.

A third watchpoint is **full-body visibility, safe space and assessability**. A useful source cluster exists around London Studio Centre, USC Kaufman, Arden, Mountview, GSA and One Dance UK safe-space guidance, but these will need disciplined extraction so that later rubric design values assessability rather than expensive production polish.

A fourth watchpoint is **accessibility-safe physicality**. Equity, Candoco, One Dance UK, RAD and ISTD all point toward access and adjustment principles, but later work must be careful not to translate these into inappropriate assumptions about capacity, stamina, flexibility or “correct” physical presentation from tape alone.

A fifth watchpoint is **category leakage**, especially the current risk that dance-only tapes inherit “vocal” or theatre-style language. The census confirms this as an audit target, but the census itself does not solve it.

A final watchpoint is **choreography pickup / learning speed**. This clearly matters in live schools/company environments, yet the public-web evidence found so far often treats it as workshop or callback behaviour. Later extraction should test whether enough tape-based evidence exists to say anything reliable beyond what is explicitly shown in the recorded task. citeturn18search0turn13search0turn27search5turn17search2turn17search9

## Reusable Handoff Pack for DANCE-B1

### Handoff summary

DANCE-S0 is complete and the Dance branch is ready to move into **DANCE-B1 extraction** using the same disciplined workflow established in the Musical Theatre pilot: source-led extraction first, then synthesis, then gap audit and revision. The B1 batch is intentionally large because official admissions and conservatoire sources are the strongest public evidence for what dance programmes actually ask applicants to demonstrate, what can be assessed from a recorded submission, and how schools distinguish present readiness from training potential.

The B1 ledger is strongest in ballet and contemporary admissions, with good additional coverage for mixed vocational pathways, commercial dance and musical-theatre-adjacent dance routes. Several schools provide explicit video or filmed-audition guidance, which is important because the current TapeCoach baseline has known dance assessability risks around full-body visibility, camera distance, safe space and over-rewarding production polish. B1 also contains several admissions-policy or audition-pack PDFs that are likely to yield higher-confidence extraction than general marketing pages, especially for selection criteria, access provisions, reasonable adjustments and process boundaries.

Important normalisation points for B1: Urdang has multiple public domains, so use the dedicated Urdang admissions pages as preferred current sources; Royal Ballet School and ENBS each have both overview pages and more detailed current-cycle pages or packs, so extract from the detailed current-cycle source first; The Place/LCDS has parallel study and course pages, both worth using because the course pages add workshop and interview specifics. Video-application pages embedded inside admissions processes, such as Central, London Studio Centre and USC Kaufman, remain B1 sources because they are part of official selection requirements, not merely generic filming advice.

When extracting B1, prioritise: explicit audition tasks, technique classes requested, solo requirements, improvisation/interview/workshop elements, what schools say they assess, what they say about potential versus current level, what is observable from a video submission, and any accessibility or support language attached directly to the admissions process.
