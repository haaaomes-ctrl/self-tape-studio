17. Reusable Handoff Pack for ACTING-REV
ACTING-AUDIT handoff summary
The Acting audit is complete. The current Run 0B Acting baseline is directionally sound but still too generic and too shared-schema-shaped to be revision-ready without additional wording and guardrails. Its strongest existing features are that it already centres Acting, recognises speech/voice as distinct from singing, names useful acting behaviours such as intention, listening, eyeline, reader relationship, thought shifts and pacing, and warns not to invent role expectations where no brief exists. However, ACTING-SYN shows that Acting cannot remain one undifferentiated standard. The revision must now make monologue, scene/duologue, screen/self-tape, stage, classical/heated text and contemporary text handling explicit inside the current framework.
The highest-priority audit issue is the reused vocal field. The baseline correctly says spoken-only work should be labelled as speech delivery rather than vocal performance, but ACTING-REV must strengthen that into a clear user-facing label boundary: spoken Acting must not be described as singing or vocal performance unless singing is actually present. Closely linked is the need to separate speech audibility from speech skill.
The second major priority is claim scope. Finished tapes can show text handling, speech clarity, camera relationship, visible choices, reader-mediated response and some physical evidence, but they cannot safely prove response to direction, group work, workshop behaviour, training potential or broader flexibility unless such evidence is directly shown or the context is admissions/live-stage. Reader availability, paid-reader quality, self-tape workload, production polish and equipment quality must also be treated as process or assessability issues, not acting merit.
The third priority is accessibility-safe assessment. ACTING-REV must add explicit non-deficit handling for access needs, visual impairment, speech difference, mobility difference, interpreters, access workers, accessible materials, extra preparation and adaptation. These are process supports, not talent deficits.
No backend, schema, score-field, weighting, cap, verdict or MT-stabilised-flow changes are supported. Physical/fight acting and comedy remain evidence-limited and should be carried as cautions rather than solved as universal rubrics.
