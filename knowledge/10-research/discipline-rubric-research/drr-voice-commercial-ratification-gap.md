# Voice rubric is filed under Commercial pending expert ratification

> **Open question / gap.** Surfacing only — these findings must **not** be incorporated into the live Commercial (or MT) rubric without expert ratification.

## Summary

The Voice rubric research ([[drr-voice-overview]]) is filed in the corpus under `discipline: commercial` with a `voice` sub-area tag. That mapping is an **operator taxonomy decision**, not a conclusion of the research. In the source files, "Voice" denotes the **sung / singing voice** (the Song / MT vocal reference — admissions songs, cuts, legit vs contemporary, art song, accompanied/unaccompanied), **not** voiceover or commercial voice. The only Voice↔Commercial relationship the research asserts is a **label-boundary**: Voice's acting-through-song semantics must not leak into the Commercial branch's distinct acting-as-presence / naturalism meaning, and "commercial" must never be read as marketability.

## Context / why

The live Commercial discipline ([[drr-commercial-overview]]) does not currently carry the Voice/singing findings, and the corpus taxonomy (Voice folded under Commercial) does not match how the research itself frames Voice (Song/MT-adjacent sung voice). Before any of this is woven into the live rubric, the discipline taxonomy needs an expert decision. This note exists to keep that decision visible rather than silently encoded in a tag.

## Open questions

- Is "Voice / singing voice" its own product discipline, a sub-area of Musical Theatre / Song, or a sub-area of Commercial?
- If Voice stays under `discipline: commercial` in the data model, how is the label-boundary (no song-semantics leakage; "commercial" ≠ marketability) enforced in the live rubric?
- Which Voice findings (if any) are ratified to inform the live Commercial or MT rubric, and which stay research-only?
