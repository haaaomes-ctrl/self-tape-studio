3. Scope and Non-Reopening Boundary
This maturity audit covers whether the completed MT branch meets the later v5 standard around traceability, output specificity, field semantics, anti-bias boundaries, source-family discipline, and live-output readiness.
It does not cover new research, new source extraction, rewritten rubric text, backend implementation, schema changes, scoring-field changes, weighting changes, cap changes, blocker changes, verdict-threshold changes, role-fit bound changes, UI design, pipeline design, or code.
A targeted MT patch is justified if the issue is already evidenced by the MT branch or supplied outputs and can be fixed through output-specificity / report-behaviour rules without reopening research.
A targeted source refresh is justified only if the desired behaviour requires new evidence, such as a complete through-composed MT framework or deeper pop-rock / belt / mix / dance-led scoring descriptors.
A broader MT v5 refresh would be justified only if the traceability chain were broken, the branch used unclear sources for major recommendations, or the revised branch conflicted with later v5 baseline standards. That is not the case here.
The following must remain preserved: acting + song detection, component breakdown, visible Acting and Vocal categories, current MT weights, six-field model, consistency_modifier, timestamp coverage across acting and song, Step 1 evidence / Step 2 polish split, fixed-material guardrails, and anti-bias / accessibility protections.

14. MT Maturity Decision
Decision: MT branch remains usable but needs an output-specificity patch before being treated as fully closed under the later v5 standard.
Rationale: the research, synthesis, audit, revision and final audit chain is strong enough. The branch does not need broader reopening, and no immediate targeted source refresh is required. The main weakness is that MT was completed before later branches established the mature standalone output-specificity and live-output QA pattern. The supplied PDFs confirm this risk: current rendered reports still contain generic MT praise, timestamp under-density, false-specificity risks, alternative repertoire advice, and role-fit / callback overclaims.
Critical blockers: MT-V5-I07 and MT-V5-I09 are the most serious live-output risks: contradictory brief/no-brief handling and readiness/verdict inconsistency. MT-V5-I01, MT-V5-I03 and MT-V5-I11 are high-priority output-quality blockers: generic acting-through-song / vocal feedback and under-produced timestamps.
Can remain closed: MT source research, synthesis, gap audit, revision, and final audit do not need reopening.
Must be patched: output-specificity rules, generic suppression, timestamp density, fixed-material boundary, no-brief false-specificity, role-fit/callback claim scope, presentation/polish handling, and live-output QA requirements.
Must be researched only if expanding scope: complete through-composed MT rules or deeper pop-rock / belt / mix / dance-led formal descriptors.
Can MT proceed into cross-branch implementation planning before the patch? No, not as a fully mature closed branch. It can proceed only if the listed MT output-specificity patch is included as a prerequisite package.
15. Reusable Handoff Pack for MT Patch or Confirmation
MT-V5 maturity handoff summary
The Musical Theatre branch is mature at research, synthesis, audit, revision and final-audit level, but not yet mature at the later v5 output-specificity standard. The original MT branch correctly preserved the stabilised MT anchor: acting + song detection, component breakdown, visible Acting and Vocal categories, MT weights, consistency_modifier, and timestamp coverage requirements. The research basis is also strong: acting-through-song, vocal technique in service of story, integration, self-tape fairness and style/subtype limitations are all traceable through source IDs and MT-A01–MT-A15.
The issue is not source maturity or rubric-slice maturity. The issue is output behaviour. The supplied PDFs show that current MT reports can still produce broad phrases such as “emotionally connected”, “strong vocal performance”, “grounded acting”, “good storytelling”, and “polished” without enough lyric-, phrase-, beat-, style- or component-specific evidence. Several outputs provide only two to four timestamped notes for multi-component MT reports, below the v5 target for clearly assessable 3–5 minute tapes. One report has a serious brief/no-brief contradiction and readiness inconsistency: it is brief-driven, says “WHY THIS ISN’T READY”, but also shows a strong verdict and later says no brief was provided. Another output gives alternative repertoire advice despite the fixed/submitted-material improvement context. These are output-specificity and QA problems, not reasons to reopen MT research.
The recommended decision is: MT remains usable but needs an output-specificity patch before cross-branch implementation planning. The patch should convert the final audit addendum into a standalone MT output-specificity handoff with adversarial tests. It should force lyric/phrase/beat evidence, distinguish vocal technique from story/style, require component-balanced timestamps, block unsupported no-brief specificity, suppress alternative-material advice in fixed-material contexts, bound role-fit / callback / workshop claims, and treat presentation as assessability rather than polish. It should also define live-output QA requirements for PDFs, JSON, comparison pages and renderer/export timestamp parity.
