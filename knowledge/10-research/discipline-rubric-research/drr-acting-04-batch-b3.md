**4. Source-by-Source Evidence Notes**

**ACTING-S035 — Equity self-tape & Zoom guidance**

The source explicitly frames itself as best-practice guidance for scripted-drama self-tapes and Zoom auditions. It says casting should give performers as much information as possible at request stage, including tone, style, period and, where possible, a brief sense of the director’s or lead creative’s vision. It treats simplicity as central: performers should not normally be asked to provide their own props or re-arrange their home as if it were a set. It also sets explicit fair-process boundaries: a four-day minimum turnaround for most self-tapes, a three-day minimum where the request is three pages or fewer, a six-page cap for a first self-tape, limits on the number of roles, scenes and versions requested, and specific weekend / Bank Holiday due-date rules. Readers are described as beneficial but not mandatory, and performers are not expected to pay for them. Translation responsibility is placed on production, not the performer, when the script needs significant translation. The page also says performers should not be asked to self-tape fully or partially nude and that casting should only request as many self-tapes as can actually be considered. The access section adds explicit inclusive-process language: Word rather than PDF documents, BSL interpreter responsibility, extra preparation time, asking all auditionees about access requirements, practical adjustments around fatigue, lighting, guides, step-free access and access-worker scheduling. No acting method is privileged. This is a strong B3 source, but it is still chiefly a process and fairness document, not a direct acting-descriptor framework. citeturn1view0

**ACTING-S036 — SAG-AFTRA self-tape guidelines**

The named member-guidance page publicly surfaces as a portal to new contract bulletins, but the page itself was blocked to direct fetch in this environment. Official SAG-AFTRA search exposure and linked official TV/theatrical materials nonetheless make several process rules explicit: breakdowns, sides and/or scripts are to be provided at least 48 hours before submission deadlines, excluding weekends and holidays; minors receive 72 hours; first self-tapes are capped at eight industry-standard pages and second or later self-tapes at twelve; performers cannot be required to memorise and may hold sides or use a prompting device; performers must be given the option of a live virtual or in-person audition instead of a self-tape; and producers cannot require a resolution above 720p, a non-free upload site, editing software or specific equipment. The same official materials also surface privacy and safety constraints: self-tapes must be stored securely, cannot be made public without performer consent, and self-tapes cannot require nudity or stunts. Method language is absent. The later caution is important: this is excellent contractual and burden-boundary evidence, but later stages must not mistake contractual process rules for direct acting-quality descriptors. citeturn0search1turn15search1turn15search2turn10search0turn9search0turn10search17turn16search1turn8search2

**ACTING-S037 — SAG-AFTRA self-tape anatomy**

The named article is publicly traceable but direct fetch was blocked and public exposure is thin. The publicly exposed official snippets still show what kind of source it is: a practitioner/process article from SAG-AFTRA Magazine which emphasises that self-taping adds labour beyond audition preparation itself because performers must prepare, set up the space, record and sometimes edit the work. The article also highlights one of the medium differences between recorded and in-person auditions: sides or cue cards are more readily accessible in self-tapes. The exposed wording also frames a self-made tape as potentially the only chance a decision-maker has to see the performer. That is useful later as process-context evidence about mediated audition conditions and overclaim risk, but the public exposure here is too thin for direct rubric language. No robust acting criteria, access rules, or methodology language are sufficiently exposed from this source in this environment, so it is correctly treated as process-only. citeturn0search2turn7search0turn12search0turn14search0
