**4. Source-by-Source Evidence Notes**

**COMMERCIAL-S015 — Code of Best Practice for Self-Tape & Zoom Auditions for Commercials**

- Explicitly frames Commercial self-tape and Zoom auditioning as a shared process problem requiring better information, lighter burden and open communication.
- Says artists should be given relevant information when a self-tape is requested, including tone, style, period or short creative vision where possible.
- Says simplicity is key: performers should not be required to provide props or re-arrange their home as though it were a set; extras should be minor and discretionary.
- Says initial requests can ask only for a basic package such as a headshot and short slate; recall-stage additions must be reasonable, role-relevant and not excessive.
- States a reader can help but must not be an absolute requirement, and actors are not expected to pay for readers.
- Says CDs should aim for a minimum of two working days for commercial self-tapes, avoid weekends and bank holidays where possible, and use extensions or abandon the tape where the rule cannot be met.
- Says scripts needing translation should be translated for the performer unless it is only a couple of lines.
- Says artists will only be judged on performance and not on the quality of equipment available to them.
- Says actors must never be asked to self-tape fully or partially nude, nor to send nude or partially nude photographs.
- Tape-observable implication: simple rooms, limited props, absent reader and modest equipment are not valid negative talent signals.
- Process-only implication: deadlines, recall fees, pencils, extensions, translation provision and data management are not performance criteria.
- Nothing in this source should become scoring evidence about resource access, speed of turnaround, reader access or production polish. citeturn29view1turn29view2turn29view0turn29view4turn29view3

**COMMERCIAL-S016 — Equity Commercial Guides / Working in Commercials**

- Gives Commercial-specific process expectations: first audition can be in person or self-tape/Zoom; self-tape/Zoom first auditions are encouraged because they widen access and reduce time/cost burden.
- Says first casting sessions should provide the advertising agency, production company, product details, purpose of the casting and intended audience.
- States artists should not be asked for detailed personal information unless absolutely essential to the role, and should be selected on suitability rather than specific personal characteristics.
- States artists should not be asked for real age, ethnicity, pregnancy or similar details before engagement, subject to limited form and insurance contexts.
- Says the ADF and recorded materials involve personal and sensitive data, and recordings/photos from casting should be destroyed after first transmission of the commercial.
- States artists should always feel safe and comfortable in casting or shooting, and should not be asked to audition nude or semi-nude.
- Includes an equality commitment based on equal opportunity and role requirements rather than stereotypical suitability, and says advertisers should use every practicable effort to facilitate an accessible venue where disabled access is inadequate.
- Tape-observable implication: the absence of expensive travel, private studios or polished production values cannot be used as a proxy for lower merit.
- Process-only implication: recall fees, travel, ADF forms, data retention, first-audition format and safe-space rules are not performance criteria.
- This source is a rights/process guide, not a performance descriptor framework. citeturn8view0turn31view1turn31view2turn31view3turn31view4turn31view5

**COMMERCIAL-S017 — CDG Code of Conduct**

- States casting should not impose unreasonable time or monetary burden and should not unreasonably affect a performer’s availability for paid work.
- Says enough time and information should be given for requests, and asks casting professionals to be mindful of a performer’s wider circumstances.
- States casting should be aware of and discuss any access needs at the earliest opportunity.
- States performers should not be asked for unnecessary personal information and should be protected from inappropriate third-party presence without knowledge and consent.
- Includes explicit boundaries on nudity and self-tapes: performers should not be asked to perform unnecessary nudity or semi-nudity in self-tapes or auditions; performers should never be asked for fully nude self-tapes or to remove modesty garments in taped submissions.
- Limits footage use and emphasises consent, permission and clarification around who will see recorded material and how it will be stored or shared.
- Tape-observable implication: personal environment, cost constraints, privacy needs and access needs must not be treated as indicators of lesser professionalism or ability.
- Process-only implication: time burden, cost burden, consent, presence of others, permissions and access arrangements are not performance criteria.
- This source is broad and ethics-led; it must not be converted into a Commercial acting standard. citeturn7view0turn7view1turn7view2turn7view3turn7view4

**COMMERCIAL-S018 — Casting Questions Card**

- Says performers should not, in most casting, audition or interview contexts, be asked questions about age, disability, gender reassignment, marriage/civil partnership, pregnancy/maternity, race, religion or belief, sex or sexual orientation.
- States protected characteristics are covered by the Equality Act 2010 and inappropriate questioning should be reported.
- Says performers must not be asked for personal information verbally or in writing before or during the audition process, including address, personal phone numbers, ethnicity and age, except where actual age must be identified for children and age-restricted products in advertising.
- Tape-observable implication: none; this is not a tape-evidence source.
- Process-only implication: protected-characteristic handling is a fairness and lawfulness boundary, not performance evidence.
- This source must not be converted into hidden eligibility scoring. citeturn42view0turn42view3

**COMMERCIAL-S019 — Advice for Visually Impaired Actors**

- Says visually impaired actors should be asked what format and access method they prefer for scripts and audition materials.
- Recommends providing text as far in advance as possible, because it may take longer to read and familiarise.
- Says line-feeding, recordings, enlarged text and screen readers may all be relevant access methods.
- Says PDFs should generally be avoided because they are hard to adapt and screen readers often cannot access them; Word documents are preferred and font size should be adjustable.
- Says workshop or practical direction should avoid vague demonstrative terms and instead describe the task clearly.
- Says navigation support, building-entry descriptions and access-worker discussion should be addressed early.
- Tape-observable implication: assistive formats, line-feeding, access workers, enlarged text and alternative handling of text must not be treated as deficits.
- Process-only implication: navigation, venue orientation, script format and access planning are non-performance issues.
- This source is access-specific and should remain an inclusion guardrail, not a hidden criterion. citeturn28view0turn28view1turn28view2turn28view3turn28view4

**COMMERCIAL-S020 — Accessible Auditions and Self Tapes for Deaf and Disabled Talent**

- Says scripts and documentation should be sent in Word format unless otherwise requested because PDFs are inaccessible for many people.
- Says working with Deaf or hard-of-hearing talent requires the casting side to source BSL interpreters where needed.
- Says many Deaf and disabled performers need extra time to prepare, read materials and organise a personal assistant or access worker, and materials should therefore be sent as far in advance as possible for both face-to-face and self-tape auditions.
- Says if performers are asked to demonstrate skills relating to their impairment, the request must be specific and honest; vague requests for “tricks” are criticised.
- Says every invitee, disabled or not, should be asked if they have access requirements, so access becomes standard process rather than exceptional treatment.
- Gives explicit examples of access requirements: step-free access, accessible toilets, guides for visually impaired performers, fatigue-managed time slots for self-taping/Zoom recalls, lighting needs for visually impaired and Deaf people, and access-worker scheduling constraints.
- Tape-observable implication: support arrangements, lighting adaptations, timing adjustments, BSL, access workers and impairment-specific clarifications must not be interpreted as weaker talent.
- Process-only implication: childcare, fatigue, venue access, interpreter sourcing and access-worker scheduling are process questions, not performance evidence.
- This source is intentionally access-led and must not be repurposed as a performance model. citeturn27view0turn27view1turn27view2turn27view3turn27view4turn27view5

**COMMERCIAL-S021 — Code of Conduct for Auditions**

- Says artists should, where possible, receive at least 48 hours’ notice of the nature of the audition, parts being cast, who will be present, whether it will be taped or recorded, and all necessary material.
- Says managers must provide as much privacy as possible and, where reasonable and practicable, access for disabled people.
- Says artists should be given reasonable time and facilities if invited to audition for an additional part not previously notified.
- Says the number of recalls should be minimised and, if recalls accumulate, travel and out-of-pocket expenses become relevant.
- Says auditions involving nudity or semi-nudity must follow the relevant schedule.
- Says performers should not be asked for personal information as part of the audition except where law allows.
- Includes advertisement-specific clauses saying audition adverts should be as specific as possible and casting breakdowns should encourage a diverse range of artists.
- Includes a self-tape clause stating artists should be given clear direction and a realistic timeframe, and there should be timely acknowledgement of receipt.
- Tape-observable implication: lateness, recall burden, travel issues, recording notice and process friction must not be treated as talent evidence.
- Process-only implication: privacy, press attendance, travel reimbursement, timeline realism and data handling are request-side or organiser-side matters.
- This is a strong general process source, but its theatre/audition ancestry means later Commercial use must stay scoped. citeturn30view3turn18view0turn18view2turn18view4turn30view0turn30view1

**COMMERCIAL-S022 — New Rules: Commercials Auditions!**

**COMMERCIAL-S024 — 2023 TV/Theatrical Contracts**

- The official SAG-AFTRA 2023 TV/Theatrical materials create a highly detailed self-tape and virtual-audition rule set that is not Commercial-specific but is highly relevant as adjacent US process evidence.
- The extractable summary says performers must receive breakdowns/sides/scripts at least 48 hours before a self-tape deadline, or 72 hours for minors, except where urgent casting makes that impossible.
- It caps pages at eight for a first self-tape and twelve for later callbacks.
- It says performers with disabilities may request materials in an accessible format.
- It says performers may not be required to memorise or be barred from holding sides or using a prompting device.
- It says performers may not be required to record above 720p, use non-free upload or meeting sites, or use editing software or specific equipment.
- It tightly limits slate requests to named items and bans fee-based general casting calls and preferential treatment based on payment to casting services.
- It prohibits nudity or over-revealing attire in self-tapes and virtual auditions, prohibits stunts, requires secure storage and legitimate-business-purpose access for recordings, and requires consent before public availability.
- It also requires opportunities for virtual or in-person interviews on a first-come, first-served basis with accommodations for performers with disabilities, senior performers and minors.
- Tape-observable implication: held sides, prompt devices, modest resolution, free platforms and simple equipment must not be scored negatively where these are contractually protected.
- Process-only implication: page caps, memorisation, upload sites, waiting time and storage/consent rules are not performance evidence.
- Limitation: this is a US TV/theatrical source, not a Commercial rulebook, so later use must stay explicitly adjacent and non-defining. citeturn37search1turn12search7turn41search15
