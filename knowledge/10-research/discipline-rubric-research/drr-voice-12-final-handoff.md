4. Branch Scope and Production Context
This branch covers Voice / Singing in Song and Musical Theatre contexts. It clarifies sung-vocal evidence, lyric storytelling through song, vocal descriptor specificity, style/subtype caution, accompaniment context, self-tape assessability, vocal-health safety and inclusive voice handling.
The revised branch outputs are intended to improve:
Song and MT sung-vocal report specificity.
Song acting/storytelling as lyric/story/communication through song.
Vocal descriptor precision for pitch, rhythm, tone, diction, phrasing, communication and interpretation.
Safety around no vocal-health diagnosis from tape.
Anti-bias handling for access needs, accent, speech difference, hearing difference, gender-diverse voice and resource limitations.
Output non-regression for Song-only, MT, Acting, Dance and Hybrid contexts.
Current live production behaviour remains separate. TapeCoach is live, but these Voice / Singing refinements are not production-shipped behaviour unless later implementation planning and rollout occur.
This branch has not implemented or shipped:
Frontend label changes.
Comparison-page changes.
Renderer / export changes.
Prompt deployment.
Report schema changes.
Backend or scoring changes.
Live-output QA for revised Voice-only behaviour.
This branch must preserve:
Six score fields.
Song and MT weights.
Server-side score recomputation.
Caps, blockers, verdict thresholds and role-fit bounds.
Step 1 / Step 2 architecture.
Locked-field enforcement.
Safety, material-policy, presentation and accessibility scrubs.
Maximum 3 strengths, maximum 3 improvements and maximum 8 timestamped notes.
Musical Theatre acting + song stabilised flow.
This branch must not change backend, schema, UI, data-flow, score fields, weights, caps, blockers, verdicts, role-fit bounds or server-side recomputation.

The strongest branch assets are its field-semantics clarification, descriptor spine, safety guardrails and output-specificity test pack. Voice / Singing clarifies that vocal means sung-vocal evidence in Song and Musical Theatre, while Acting / Monologue and Dance retain their non-singing proxy meanings. Song remains vocal-centred but must include interpretive evidence where assessable: lyric intention, communication, phrase action, emotional arc, character or song situation, and acting-through-song. Formal frameworks supplied vocabulary for pitch, rhythm, tone, diction, phrasing, communication and interpretation, but external marks, grades and percentages remain excluded.
The branch also separates audio/technical assessability from vocal merit, treats accompaniment and self-accompaniment as task/context-specific, blocks production polish and paid-resource access as talent evidence, preserves fixed-material policy, and suppresses generic vocal praise unless anchored to observable evidence. B4 closes the safety gap: no vocal-health diagnosis from tape. Strain-like, fatigued or hoarse sound may be described only as cautious observation. Access needs, disability, neurodivergence, hearing difference, accent, speech difference, gender-diverse voice, assistive technology and reasonable adjustments are non-deficit context.
Residual limitations are explicit: belt, mix and registration evidence is thin; jazz, folk and broad commercial-pop descriptors are uneven; actor-musician evidence is partial; no Voice-only live outputs exist; frontend labels, comparison-page parity, rendered timestamp parity, score stability, implementation behaviour and rollout effects remain unverified.
